Deeper study · Chapter 10

Science, Evidence, and Explanation

For when a section didn't click, or left you wanting more: each page explains the idea again, tells the full story, fills in what the chapter leaves out, and gives the sources.

Joseph Wright of Derby, An Experiment on a Bird in the Air Pump, 1768

← Back to Chapter 10

  • Short study · 3 minWhat makes science special?Science combines ways of asking questions, producing evidence, and correcting mistakes. Its methods vary with the subject and the problem.
  • Short study · 3 minThe demarcation problemDemarcation asks how science should be distinguished from other activities. Distinguishing science from pseudoscience is one part of that question.
  • Short study · 3 minInductivism and its limitsScientific generalization involves more than collecting repeated observations. Researchers must decide what to observe, how to classify it, and which pattern deserves projection.
  • Short study · 3 minPopper and falsificationismA theory has empirical content when it rules out some possible observations. Popper makes attempts to expose such failures central to science.
  • Short study · 3 minThe Duhem-Quine problemA test usually depends on a theory together with assumptions about the instrument, the sample, and the setting. If its prediction fails, at least one part of that package needs reconsideration.
  • Short study · 3 minAd hoc hypothesesA failed prediction can prompt a useful new hypothesis or an evasive repair. The timing of the proposal does not decide which it is.
  • Full study · 11 minKuhn and paradigmsScientific work needs shared examples of what counts as a good problem and a good solution. Kuhn called attention to how those examples organize research, rather than treating science as a list of independently tested propositions.
  • Short study · 3 minLakatos and research programmesLakatos evaluates research programmes that generate sequences of related theories. A programme protects shared core commitments while revising auxiliary hypotheses.
  • Short study · 3 minFeyerabend and LaudanFeyerabend challenges the claim that one exceptionless method governs scientific progress. Laudan challenges strong conclusions drawn from the mere possibility of alternative theories.
  • Short study · 3 minInference to the best explanationAn explanation can support a hypothesis without proving it. Inference to the best explanation compares candidates by how well they would account for the evidence.
  • Short study · 3 minTheory-ladenness of observationAn observation depends on choices about what to notice, measure, and describe. Scientific concepts and expectations help organize those choices.
  • Full study · 12 minUnderdeterminationEvidence may leave more than one theory possible, but possibility is weaker than equal support. Underdetermination concerns both which part of a tested package should be revised and which rival package should be accepted.
  • Short study · 3 minScientific realism and anti-realismA successful theory can predict observations while also describing things we cannot directly observe. Scientific realism gives a positive epistemic interpretation to at least some of those descriptions.
  • Full study · 11 minCausation and causal inferenceA useful prediction does not automatically tell you what would happen if you changed the predictor. Causal inference asks about that change and needs assumptions about how the system works.
  • Short study · 3 minValues in scienceValues can affect which questions science investigates and how it handles uncertain conclusions. That influence is not identical to changing results to fit a preference.
  • Short study · 3 minScientific consensusAgreement among relevant experts can be evidence for someone who cannot assess every underlying result. Its force depends on how the agreement formed and what exactly is agreed upon.
  • Short study · 3 minThe replication crisisA published result needs more scrutiny than its initial analysis. Repeating calculations, collecting new data with similar methods, and testing a hypothesis in a different way check different things.
  • Short study · 3 minPseudoscience and how to spot itScientific pretence can imitate journals, conferences, and technical language. To assess a claim, inspect how its proponents obtain evidence and respond to criticism.
