> **In short.** A failed prediction can prompt a useful new hypothesis or an evasive repair. The timing of the proposal does not decide which it is. Ask whether the repair has independent support and exposes the revised account to further tests. A modification that accommodates every outcome can weaken the account's empirical content. A modification that explains the mismatch and predicts something new can advance inquiry.

## Re-learn

### Two repairs to the same invented failure

Suppose a model predicts that an imaginary alloy conducts electricity, but one sample does not. One repair says that this particular sample is an exception for no specified reason. Another says a particular contaminant blocks conduction and predicts that its removal will restore it.

Both claims can accommodate the failed test. The second also connects the discrepancy to an independently investigable feature. Tests for the contaminant, trials with purified samples, and deliberately contaminated samples could count against it. The first repair has supplied no comparable route to failure.

Popper's criticism of ad hoc modifications concerns their capacity to save a theory while undermining its testability. Hepburn and Andersen also describe his later recognition that modifications prompted by anomalies can be scientifically productive. Their planetary example shows why a proposal made in response to a discrepancy is not automatically an illegitimate rescue. [@hepburn-andersen2026, § 3.3]

### More than a story that fits

A useful question is what the repair contributes besides consistency with the result that prompted it. Does it explain other evidence? Does it rely on a mechanism already supported elsewhere? Does it generate a contrast with rival proposals? A detailed answer can guide further inquiry even before the repair has been established.

Lakatos evaluates sequences of theories within research programmes. A modification is theoretically progressive when it solves a difficulty while generating independently testable new predictions; empirical progress requires confirmation of at least some of those predictions. That distinction makes room for promising work without calling every proposed repair a success. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.4]

## Beyond the chapter

### Avoid judging only by timing or complexity

The ordinary phrase *made up after the fact* bundles different criticisms. A hypothesis might be newly invented but independently testable. Another might have been proposed long before the test yet be so flexible that every outcome can be accommodated. The relevant problem is the evidential role of the proposal, not merely its date.

Complexity is also insufficient for a verdict. A simple stipulation can evade evidence, while a complicated model can make demanding predictions. Evaluating a revision requires looking at the constraints it introduces and the support it receives. Laudan's critique of underdetermination highlights why fitting a record is weaker than being equally well supported. [@stanford2023, § 2.2]

### A repaired package remains a package

If the purified alloy still fails to conduct, perhaps the contamination account is wrong. Perhaps purification failed. Perhaps another assumption failed. The [Duhem–Quine problem](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/the-duhem-quine-problem) applies to the new test too. Independent checks reduce the available excuses; they do not turn testing into a process without assumptions.

This does not make evaluation hopeless. It shifts attention to patterns of evidence across tests. A repair that repeatedly requires unverified exceptions deserves a different assessment from one that survives independent checks and supports useful predictions. Lakatos's account provides one way to appraise that history. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.4]

The chapter's criticism of ad hoc reasoning should therefore target insulation from evidence. It should not become a prohibition on revising assumptions when results are surprising. See [Popper](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/popper-and-falsificationism) for testability and [research programmes](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/lakatos-and-research-programmes) for the longer view.
