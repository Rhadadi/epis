> **In short.** Lakatos evaluates research programmes that generate sequences of related theories. A programme protects shared core commitments while revising auxiliary hypotheses. That protection can support progress if the revisions yield new testable consequences. It becomes degenerating when the sequence fails to make the relevant theoretical or empirical progress. The distinction evaluates a history of inquiry rather than supplying an immediate rule for every research decision.

## Re-learn

### A sequence instead of one sentence

Imagine a research team proposing several models of an invented mechanism. Every model uses the same central principle, but each changes assumptions about friction or measurement. When one prediction fails, the team revises those assumptions and tests the next model.

Lakatos's *hard core* consists of commitments protected within the programme. The *protective belt* contains auxiliaries open to revision. The programme also has heuristic guidance: revisions should be directed by its resources rather than made at random. Musgrave and Pigden present this as an attempt to mediate between Popper's emphasis on criticism and Kuhn's emphasis on sustained commitment. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.4]

Calling the core protected does not mean it has been proved true. It describes the strategy of that programme. Investigators can compare the performance of the resulting sequence with the sequences produced by competing programmes. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.4]

### Two forms of progress

A new model can be theoretically progressive if it handles the earlier difficulty and generates new, independently testable predictions. Empirical progress requires that at least some of those predictions receive confirmation. The second requirement is stronger than merely announcing an ingenious possibility. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.4]

In the imaginary mechanism case, a friction hypothesis might predict a previously unmeasured change at a different speed. Producing the prediction is one achievement; finding that change under an independent test is another. A programme can retain unresolved problems while making these gains.

Lakatos therefore does not demand that an isolated theory be free of every apparent refutation before its programme counts as progressive. He also differs from a picture of science normally organized around one dominant paradigm: competing programmes play a central role in his account. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.4]

## Beyond the chapter

### Appraisal and abandonment are different decisions

A programme's present difficulties are evidence about its performance, not a proof that it can never recover. Musgrave and Pigden discuss a historical reversal in appraisals of competing research concerning intelligence, illustrating how a programme assessed as degenerating can later gain ground. That fallibility matters when using the label to decide what should receive further investigation. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.5]

The same caution applies to a successful programme. Progress is an assessment of a sequence and its evidential achievements. It does not automatically establish every core commitment as literally true. The wider issue belongs to [scientific realism](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/scientific-realism-and-anti-realism).

### History can test a methodology, but also flatter it

Lakatos evaluates accounts of rationality partly through how well they reconstruct significant scientific history. Musgrave and Pigden raise the danger that an account could lose its critical force by treating whatever successful scientists did as rational. A historical reconstruction needs standards that permit criticism, rather than just a favorable retelling. [@musgrave-pigden2026, § 3.5]

This leaves a demanding task: explain when sustained commitment produces new knowledge and when it chiefly accommodates failure. The framework helps state that task over time, but applying it requires a specific record of predictions, results, and revisions.

See [ad hoc hypotheses](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/ad-hoc-hypotheses) for evaluating repairs and [Kuhn](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/kuhn-and-paradigms) for the competing account of normal research and revolutionary change.
