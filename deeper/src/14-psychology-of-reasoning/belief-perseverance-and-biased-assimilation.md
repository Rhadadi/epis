> **In short.** Belief perseverance concerns retaining a belief after its original grounds are undermined. Biased assimilation concerns interpreting evidence through an existing position. The two can reinforce one another, but they are different patterns. A correction requires tracing what depended on the withdrawn information. It does not require reversing every conclusion regardless of independent support.

## Re-learn

### A withdrawn report and the story it generated

Imagine a fictional manager told that a new packaging method reduced breakage. She explains the result by saying the new shape protects corners better. Later, the original report is revealed to contain a data-entry error. The explanation still sounds plausible, but it was constructed partly to explain a result that no longer stands.

The manager should separate independent evidence for the mechanism from a story generated by the mistaken report. Coherence alone does not make that story a new observation. On the other hand, if an independent test supports the method, withdrawing one report need not defeat the whole conclusion.

Mercier and Sperber review belief-perseverance experiments involving feedback later revealed to be bogus. In a reviewed study, favourable and unfavourable feedback are treated differently after debriefing. They interpret that asymmetry as evidence for a motivated mechanism, rather than mere inertial attachment to the first information. [@mercier-sperber2011, PDF p. 12]

### Assimilation happens during evaluation

Biased assimilation concerns how information is assessed before any retraction. The target article reviews Lord and colleagues’ study in which supporters and opponents of the death penalty evaluate conflicting evidence differently according to its conclusion. It reports selective treatment of methodological strengths and weaknesses. This is a secondary account of the original experiment. [@mercier-sperber2011, PDF pp. 10–11]

For the manager, assimilation would occur if she called a small sample acceptable when it favoured the new packaging but disqualifying when it favoured the old one, without a relevant reason. The specific asymmetry is more informative than the general accusation that she refuses to listen.

## Beyond the chapter

### Persistence is not always irrational

People can retain a conclusion because other grounds remain, because the alleged correction is unreliable, or because the change concerns only a peripheral premise. Those are possible explanations to investigate. Self-deception theories distinguish motivated treatment of contrary evidence from innocent error, and dispute how intentions and beliefs enter that process. [@deweeseboyd2023, § 3.1]

In the fictional case, an argument map would show which claims depended on the mistaken breakage report. If the explanation of corner protection had no separate evidence, its standing should change. If a materials test independently supports it, that support should remain visible. The correction concerns a network of reasons, not a ritual of abandoning whatever was previously believed.

### Do not turn one reviewed result into a universal law

The experimental review illustrates possible mechanisms in particular tasks. It does not establish that presenting balanced evidence generally polarises every audience or that corrections usually backfire. Such stronger claims would need their own evidence about populations, conditions, and measured outcomes.

Replication research distinguishes repeating methods from successfully reproducing an outcome, and direct from conceptual replication. Those distinctions matter when extending a finding from a laboratory feedback task to public disputes. A related-looking phenomenon is not automatically the same measured effect. [@fidler-wilcox2026, § 1]

See [motivated reasoning](deeper:14-psychology-of-reasoning/motivated-reasoning) for the possible role of preference and [misinformation](deeper:12-social-epistemology/misinformation-disinformation-and-propaganda) for correction in communication. Keeping source reliability, inferential dependence, and motivation separate makes a correction easier to evaluate.
