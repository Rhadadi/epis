> **In short.** Inquiry takes place from a position with particular information, skills, and interests. Acknowledging that position can help expose limitations. It does not establish that every perspective is equally accurate. Objectivity can concern the subject, the assessment, or the procedure. A useful account explains how situated judgments become answerable to evidence and criticism.

## Re-learn

### Two observers, one target

Imagine two people examining a fictional building. One knows its wiring; the other knows how wheelchair users navigate it. They can identify different features without offering contradictory descriptions. Combining relevant information can improve an assessment, provided the information is accurate and its relevance explained.

Now imagine they disagree about whether a particular door opens. Their different interests do not establish that both descriptions are correct. They need to specify the conditions, compare evidence, and investigate the disagreement. Perspective explains something about the route to a judgment; it does not alone determine its truth.

Baghramian and Carter distinguish strong relativity of truth values from a weaker perspectival thesis about truths available through particular concepts or forms of life. A truth visible from one angle need not thereby be false from another. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.2]

### Objectivity has different jobs

Reiss and Sprenger's account distinguishes impartiality, neutrality, and autonomy in debates about value-free science. Their discussion shows why an inquiry can fail one proposed ideal without every result becoming unusable. Agenda, assessment, and the content of theories raise different questions. [@reiss-sprenger2020, § 3.1]

In the building example, concern about accessibility can select a question. That concern does not settle whether the door opens. An assessment becomes clearer when it communicates both the reason for asking and the evidence for the answer.

## Beyond the chapter

### Criticism rather than a pretend absence of position

Feminist epistemologies examine how exclusion and neglected questions can damage inquiry. Reiss and Sprenger discuss examples and the attempts to formulate a value-laden science that remains reliable and objective. They distinguish these projects from stronger standpoint claims and note objections to them. [@reiss-sprenger2020, § 5.2]

Longino's contextual empiricism emphasizes opportunities for criticism, shared standards, uptake, and intellectual authority among qualified participants. A perspective contributes to objectivity when it can expose assumptions and receive a substantive response, rather than merely being displayed alongside others. [@reiss-sprenger2020, § 5.2]

In the fictional building review, inviting an accessibility specialist matters if their observations can change the evaluation. A meeting that records their comments and then ignores every relevant objection has not supplied the same safeguard.

### Diversity needs a connection to the question

Different perspectives can contribute different information. They can also share an error or disagree for reasons unrelated to the target. Their number does not measure accuracy. The useful questions concern the information supplied, its quality, and the process for addressing conflicts.

Standpoint proposals can make stronger claims about epistemic benefits associated with social position. Those claims require assessment rather than an assumption that every marginalized or dominant position yields the same advantages in every domain. Reiss and Sprenger present the proposed benefits together with criticisms of their scope and institutional implications. [@reiss-sprenger2020, § 5.2]
