> **In short.** Relativism makes a claim about what depends on which standard or framework. Different beliefs, different evidence, and different truth values are not the same kind of dependence. A global thesis faces problems different from a local theory of taste or justification. Contemporary truth relativists distinguish the context in which a claim is made from the context in which it is assessed. Evaluating the view requires identifying that machinery before applying a familiar objection.

## Re-learn

### First identify the disagreement

Imagine two people saying that an invented hill is steep. One is discussing a walking route; the other is designing a railway. Their standards of relevance may differ. Now imagine they disagree about its measured incline. That is another dispute, and neither person's preferred use changes the recorded angle.

The example separates a contextual description from a claim that truth itself is relative. Baghramian and Carter begin by asking what is relativized and what it is relativized to. The target could be truth, justification, or value; the parameter could be a person, culture, historical period, conceptual framework, or context of assessment. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.1]

### Four claims that need different arguments

People believe different things. Their evidence can differ. Their standards for evaluating evidence can differ. A proposition's truth might depend on an assessor's standard. The first three claims do not automatically establish the fourth. The entry distinguishes empirical diversity, epistemic relativism, and contemporary truth-relativist semantics. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.1, 4.4, 5.1]

Likewise, a sentence can express different propositions on different occasions without the same proposition changing truth value between assessors. If two people report being tired, the sentence refers to a different person each time. Contemporary relativists acknowledge this familiar contextualism but propose a further kind of dependence. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.1]

### Scope comes before the verdict

A local relativist about taste need not relativize every scientific or logical claim. A global relativist has a wider burden, including explaining how the thesis applies to its own statement. One objection to a global slogan cannot automatically refute every local view. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.1]

## The full story

### A family of theses

The label *relativism* covers different positions, which is why a useful assessment starts with a dependency claim. Baghramian and Carter's covariance definition asks both what varies and what it varies with. They also discuss negative definitions, based on rejecting absolutism, objectivism, monism, or particular kinds of realism. These approaches overlap without making every view identical. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.1, 1.2]

Consider three invented claims. A speed limit depends on the jurisdiction. A judgment that a meal is delicious depends on a standard of taste. A claim about the number of plates on the table depends on how many plates there are. All three involve some dependence, but they do not support one general theory merely by sharing the word *depends*.

Even a measurement's numerical expression can depend on a scale without supporting a controversial relativism. Different temperature scales need not describe different physical conditions. The philosophical question is which dependencies concern the authority, truth, or correctness of a judgment and whether an appropriate comparison remains available. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.1]

### The hidden-parameter proposal

One approach treats an apparently simple predicate as involving an unstated parameter. A judgment of rightness, rationality, or truth is reconstructed as a judgment relative to a code, epistemic system, or framework. The proposed parameter makes explicit what the relativist thinks the original language concealed. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.3]

This proposal needs to do more than describe what a group believes. Saying that a fictional club regards a meal as delicious is a report of its attitude. Saying that the meal is delicious relative to a standard is a claim evaluated under the proposed semantics. A theory must explain that difference rather than using *true for* as an unexplained substitute for *believed by*. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3, 4.3.1]

The same distinction matters for institutional facts. A rule can apply only within a particular institution while its application is objectively assessable once the institution and circumstances are specified. The mere need to specify a parameter does not establish every stronger relativist conclusion.

### Global and local scope

A global thesis extends relativization across beliefs regardless of subject matter. Local theories restrict it to a domain such as taste or ethics. Baghramian and Carter note that local approaches are more prominent in contemporary debates and need not include their own statement within the relativized domain. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.1]

An invented argument about taste might conclude that some taste claims depend on an assessor. The philosophical argument itself need not be a taste claim. It can therefore be assessed as an argument about semantics without assuming that its own conclusion is only tasty or untasty for its audience.

That does not establish the local view. It explains why a global self-reference objection may miss its target. To assess the local view, one should investigate its proposed domain, its account of disagreement, and the ordinary linguistic behavior it is meant to explain. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.1, 5.1]

### Strong and weak relativity

Baghramian and Carter distinguish a strong thesis, on which the same judgment can be true under one framework and false under another, from a weaker thesis concerning availability or expressibility. On the weaker account, a truth may require concepts available only within a form of life without becoming false from another angle. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.2]

The distinction helps with talk of perspectives. An expert can see a pattern a novice cannot recognize. A culture can have concepts another has not learned. Neither fact alone shows that the expert's judgment is false for the novice or that the culturally specific truth lacks an appropriate application elsewhere.

Thus an argument about the limits of conceptual access need not be an argument for contradictory truth values. If the claimed difference concerns what someone can understand, say so. If it concerns what is true, supply the further account.

### Diversity and disagreement

Empirical differences in beliefs and values motivate many relativist arguments. Baghramian and Carter emphasize that the fact of diversity does not logically entail philosophical relativism. An anti-relativist can accept disagreement and attribute error; a relativist seeks a different account of what the diversity reveals. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.1]

Imagine two fictional communities with different explanations of seasonal floods. Their disagreement does not by itself establish that both explanations are true. Additional evidence might support one, undermine both, or reveal that the communities were answering different questions. The observed disagreement leaves those possibilities open.

Persistent disagreement is a stronger challenge than one initial difference, but persistence still needs interpretation. Are the parties equally informed? Do they use the same concepts? Have they examined the same counterarguments? A thesis about intractability needs more than the observation that the dispute has lasted a long time. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.2]

There is also a challenge for the relativist's explanation of disagreement. If each party merely reports correctness under a different code, they might assert compatible propositions. The theory must explain why their exchange still seems to concern one disputed subject. Contemporary truth relativism develops this issue explicitly. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.2, 5.1]

### A primary text: Plato turns Protagoras's claim around

In the *Theaetetus*, Socrates challenges the doctrine associated with Protagoras by considering the judgments of its opponents. The following question appears in the discussion conventionally located at 171a–c. The wording is from the Jowett translation available in the corpus. [@plato-theaetetus, 171a–c; @baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3.1]

::: original The opponent's judgment
> And does he not allow that his own opinion is false, if he admits that the opinion of those who think him false is true?
:::
[@plato-theaetetus, 171a–c]. Socrates asks whether granting every person's judgment the proposed standing forces Protagoras to grant the judgment against his doctrine.

The argument is not simply a majority vote. Its important move concerns the doctrine's own treatment of the opponent's belief. If it gives that belief truth, and the belief rejects the doctrine, how can the doctrine retain its proposed universal standing? [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3.1]

::: argument The self-application problem
1. The doctrine grants the relevant truth to everyone's judgments.
2. Some people judge the doctrine false.
3. The doctrine must therefore grant the relevant truth to that rejection.
4. Its defender owes an explanation of how this is compatible with maintaining the doctrine.
:::
[@plato-theaetetus, 171a–c; @baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3.1]. The phrase *relevant truth* marks the question on which the objection and the reply can diverge.

### Logical and dialectical objections differ

A relativist can reply that the doctrine is false relative to an opponent's framework while true relative to their own. Baghramian and Carter discuss the charge that a quick refutation assumes the absolute truth conception under dispute. They also explain why avoiding a simple contradiction leaves a further problem about argument and persuasion. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3.1]

If the defender offers reasons that should persuade an opponent, what standing do those reasons have across frameworks? Saying the opponent has no reason to accept the doctrine is not equivalent to showing the doctrine internally contradictory. Yet it can undermine a proposed public defense. The logical and dialectical burdens should stay separate. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3.1]

The entry also considers more sophisticated formulations and defenses, including approaches that make truth relative without treating every actual belief as automatically correct. These replies introduce commitments that need examination. They should not be dismissed as the same unrestricted belief-to-truth rule that the original argument attacks. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.3.1]

The productive question is therefore which version survives which objection. Showing that one formulation is coherent does not establish its plausibility. Showing that another formulation contradicts itself does not eliminate the entire family.

### No neutral ground

A major motivation for epistemic relativism is the apparent absence of a framework-neutral justification of fundamental standards. Every argument uses some starting points and inferential norms. A defender of a rival standard may reject exactly the resources used to assess it. Baghramian and Carter reconstruct this motivation and its critics. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.3]

Imagine two fictional inquirers disputing whether a divination method supplies evidence about the weather. The defender may reject an assessment that assumes observational checks have superior authority. But the fact that an assessment uses disputed standards does not automatically establish that no method is more reliable or that every method is equally warranted.

::: argument An inference requiring another premise
1. We cannot justify standards without using some standards.
2. Therefore no fully neutral justification is available in the required sense.
3. It does not follow without further argument that every competing standard is equally valid.
:::
[@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.3, 4.4]. The dispute concerns what the absence of neutrality establishes, not whether reasoning can proceed without any norms.

The problem connects to the [criterion](deeper:06-justification/the-problem-of-the-criterion) and the [regress of justification](deeper:06-justification/the-regress-problem). Theories that address those problems offer different accounts of reasonable starting points. None is adopted merely by renaming the difficulty relativism.

### Boghossian and his critics

Baghramian and Carter reconstruct Boghossian's criticism of epistemic relativism: a view denying absolute facts about what justifies what must still explain how an agent accepts an epistemic system and how the view can be formulated coherently. His Galileo–Bellarmine example illustrates supposedly competing grids for assessing evidence. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.4]

The example functions here as a philosophical comparison through the secondary source. It should not be mistaken for a complete primary reconstruction of the historical controversy. The question concerns what would follow if the agents' standards were opposed in the relevant way.

Wright objects that Boghossian's reconstruction can conflate making a judgment under a standard with making a report about what the standard mandates. Baghramian and Carter also describe controversies within hinge epistemology over alternative fundamental commitments and incommensurability. The literature contains disputes about both the thesis and its adequate characterization. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 4.4]

This makes precision useful. An agent who judges that the weather will change need not consciously judge that their epistemic framework licenses the prediction. A theory that replaces the former with the latter must explain why that reconstruction preserves the original act.

### Context of use and context of assessment

Contemporary New Relativism separates familiar context-sensitive content from assessment-sensitive truth. A contextualist can treat two taste utterances as expressing different propositions tied to their speakers' standards. A truth relativist instead seeks to preserve one proposition that the speakers affirm and deny, with truth assessed relative to a further standard. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.1]

Imagine two people discussing an invented sauce. One finds it delicious; the other denies that it is delicious. A contextualist can reconstruct compatible reports about their separate tastes. The relativist argues that this loses the apparent shared content of their disagreement and proposes an assessor parameter to preserve it. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.1]

The additional parameter is meant to allow *faultless disagreement*: one content is affirmed and denied, yet each assessment can be correct relative to its relevant standard. This is an explanatory proposal about certain discourse, not a declaration that contradictory laboratory measurements are equally correct. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.1]

The ordinary indexical example of two people saying they are hungry does not by itself refute this proposal. The relativist already distinguishes variation in expressed content from variation in assessment. A critic must address the additional machinery or argue that the alleged disagreement does not require it. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.1]

### Assertion and communication remain constraints

One objection asks what an assertor aims at if the utterance can be correct relative to one assessor and incorrect relative to another. Another asks how assertion transfers a belief to an audience when the asserted content has different standing for the audience. Baghramian and Carter discuss both challenges and attempts to answer them. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.6.1]

MacFarlane's defended norm treats assertion as undertaking a commitment to defend the claim or withdraw it if a challenge cannot be met. This is one proposal for accommodating assessment sensitivity within a continuing practice of accountability. Whether it adequately explains assertion remains disputed. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 5.6.1]

The important point for evaluation is that semantics does not work alone. A successful theory must also make sense of correction, denial, disagreement, and the responsibilities speakers undertake. Adding a parameter is a technical move whose explanatory consequences still need assessment.

### Tolerance and relativism

Tolerance motivates some defenses of relativism, but the connection is contested. Critics argue that respecting people does not require endorsing every belief or making every claim relative. They also ask whether a global relativism could give tolerance universal authority. Defenders of more local views distinguish their ethical purposes from a global thesis. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 2.6]

In an invented discussion, one can allow both participants to speak, seek their reasons, and protect them from coercion while still concluding that one account of the flood is false. Those are different judgments about treatment of people and assessment of propositions.

Conversely, rejecting relativism does not establish one's own infallibility. A person can believe there is a correct answer while remaining uncertain whether they have found it. Cultural humility and careful inquiry therefore need not wait for agreement on the metaphysics of truth.

::: positions Questions that identify the actual thesis
| View | Dependence proposed | Question to press |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | Beliefs vary across communities. | What follows beyond that observed variation? |
| Contextualist | Context helps fix expressed content. | Are the parties asserting compatible propositions? |
| Epistemic | Warrant depends on a framework. | What establishes the absence of superior standards? |
| Truth relativist | One content is assessed under different parameters. | Does this explain disagreement and assertion? |
| Global | Relativization covers every relevant claim. | How does it handle its own assertion? |
:::
[@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.1, 2.1, 4.3.1, 4.4, 5.1, 5.6.1]. These questions distinguish theories rather than deciding them by a slogan.

## Beyond the chapter

### What the quick refutations leave out

The self-refutation argument is a serious challenge to global formulations, with a long history. Its application depends on the proposed truth relation and the scope of the thesis. Local theories and assessment-sensitive semantics need their own objections. The entry discusses those replies alongside their remaining burdens. [@baghramian-carter2025, § 1.4.1, 4.3.1, 5.1]

The T-schema alone also does not settle every philosophical option. It does not decide the meaning of every embedded predicate or the appropriate parameters for evaluation. [Theories of truth](deeper:11-truth-and-relativism/theories-of-truth) explains its status as an adequacy constraint rather than a universal verdict.

[Underdetermination](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/underdetermination) distinguishes logical compatibility from equal evidential support. [Social constructionism](deeper:11-truth-and-relativism/social-constructionism) separates classifications, objects, and their dependence on practices. [Perspectivism](deeper:11-truth-and-relativism/perspectivism-and-objectivity) asks how situated inquiry can remain accountable.
