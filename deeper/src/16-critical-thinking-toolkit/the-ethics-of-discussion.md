> **In short.** Fair discussion makes it possible to state, challenge and revise a position. Participants should address the claim actually made, disclose consequential assumptions, and acknowledge what an answer establishes. Courtesy alone does not make an argument reasonable. A discussion's purpose affects which moves are appropriate. Ending an unproductive exchange can be justified without declaring either position true.

## Re-learn

### Protect the possibility of an answer

Imagine a residents' meeting about closing a communal garden. If supporters can speak but opponents cannot question the attendance figures, the discussion cannot properly assess that premise. If every challenge is treated as hostility, a factual dispute has lost its route to resolution.

Hansen's account of pragma-dialectics presents critical discussion as a normative ideal, including freedom to advance views, responsibility to defend them, clear formulations and acknowledgment of the outcome. It evaluates failures that can concern conduct as well as inference. These are standards proposed by a theory, rather than a report that actual discussions always follow them. [@hansen2024b, § 3.6]

In the fictional meeting, an appropriate repair could give both sides access to the same figures, a chance to question the counting method, and an agreed way to record unresolved matters. A polite chair who denies those opportunities still impedes inquiry.

### Concede the part established

If reliable records show that attendance declined, acknowledge that fact even if you oppose closure. The recommendation also depends on costs, benefits and alternatives. Preserving that distinction lets agreement on one premise narrow the dispute without forcing agreement on every conclusion.

Conversely, a proponent should not treat agreement about declining attendance as consent to close the garden. Address the remaining premises. Ethical conduct includes reporting the other person's concessions accurately, rather than enlarging them for rhetorical advantage.

## Beyond the chapter

### State what kind of exchange this is

An inquiry asks what the records show. Deliberation asks what to do. Negotiation may seek an arrangement acceptable to different groups. Hansen's review contrasts the pragma-dialectical ideal with Walton's recognition of different dialogue frameworks and purposes. The standards of a factual inquiry cannot simply be replaced by splitting the difference in a negotiation. [@hansen2024b, § 3.6]

The residents might negotiate alternating access days without agreeing about every value judgment. That compromise would not establish the exact attendance figure by averaging their claims. State which disagreement has been resolved and which has merely been accommodated.

### Obligations have practical limits

A participant can request clarification in good faith while lacking the knowledge to formulate a precise objection. Make a reasonable effort to explain. Another participant may repeat answered demands without engaging the replies. Point to what has already been supplied and identify any genuinely new question.

Nobody's finite time becomes unlimited because a claim is contestable. Pausing or ending an exchange can preserve resources for a better setting. It does not establish that the other person is wrong or that the departing participant had no answer. See [rhetorical tactics](deeper:15-fallacies/rhetorical-tactics-that-corrupt-discussion).

Hitchcock treats maintaining focus and abandoning unproductive strategies as internal dispositions of critical thinking. Those ideals can support changing the form of a discussion when its current form blocks progress. [@hitchcock2024, § 8.2]

Finally, correction should be concrete. Identify what changed, what remains supported and why. An admission of error matters because it revises a claim, not because it performs humility. See [epistemic humility](deeper:13-virtues-and-ethics-of-belief/epistemic-humility-and-confidence) for the relationship between confidence and responsiveness to reasons.
