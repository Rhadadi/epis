> **In short.** Understanding begins by identifying the claim actually made. Preserve its scope, qualifications and purpose when restating it. A stronger alternative can help test an idea, but it should be marked as your reconstruction. Ask for clarification where different readings change the argument. Charity improves interpretation when it keeps both fairness and fidelity in view.

## Re-learn

### Keep the qualifications

Imagine a neighbour saying that a bus service is unreliable on winter weekends. Restating this as a claim that public transport never works changes the season, days and breadth of the conclusion. Refuting the broader claim would leave the neighbour's observation untouched.

A faithful restatement identifies the service, the period, and what reliability means: cancellations, arrival variation, or whether a journey can be completed. It may also distinguish a complaint about experienced inconvenience from a general claim about every scheduled journey. Ask which reading the neighbour intended before applying a statistical test to a different one.

The IEP straw-man entry explains the error of attacking an easily refuted position that the opponent would not endorse. Groarke's account of standardising arguments identifies clarifying incomplete or ambiguous claims as part of reconstruction. [@fallacy-iep, Straw Man; @groarke2026, § 3]

### Interpret without silently improving

Suppose the neighbour offers only two bad journeys. You can reconstruct a modest claim that these particular journeys were disrupted, or consider a stronger argument using the service's full cancellation record. The second is a useful investigation, but its additional evidence was not supplied by the neighbour.

Keep those activities separate. Report the stated argument, then identify the stronger version you are considering. This protects the speaker from distortion and protects the reader from believing that assumptions or evidence were actually offered when they were supplied by the interpreter.

Groarke discusses charity in interpreting implicit components, including choosing a logically minimal premise that connects the stated reasons to the conclusion. That is a reason to expose the bridge, not to manufacture whatever premise would make a desired conclusion inevitable. [@groarke2026, § 3.1]

## Beyond the chapter

### Find the function of a sentence

A statement can be a premise, conclusion, objection, explanation or example. Its role depends on context. Someone might describe an alarming possibility to argue for investigating it, rather than assert that it has occurred. Restoring that purpose can change the apparent disagreement.

Groarke notes that inference indicators are helpful without being necessary or sufficient: words such as *because* can also appear in explanations and other constructions. Understanding an argument requires more than extracting sentences following particular words. [@groarke2026, § 3]

In the bus case, an explanation of why yesterday's journey failed is different from evidence that next Sunday's will fail. Both can matter, but they answer different questions. Identify the time and target before deciding whether the supporting reason fits.

### Agreement on wording leaves assessment open

Confirming that your restatement is accurate does not make it true. Nor does disagreement about the restatement establish that the other person is evasive. They may be distinguishing a prediction from a recommendation or rejecting a stronger quantifier you introduced.

A useful interpretation records remaining alternatives and explains why they matter. If the speaker is unavailable, prefer a reading supported by the full passage and mark consequential uncertainty. Compare [ambiguity](deeper:15-fallacies/fallacies-of-ambiguity) for errors created by unnoticed shifts of meaning.

Once the claim is stable enough to assess, move to [the kind of disagreement](deeper:16-critical-thinking-toolkit/step-2-identify-the-kind-of-disagreement). Understanding is complete for present purposes when the next step addresses a claim the available words and context reasonably support.
