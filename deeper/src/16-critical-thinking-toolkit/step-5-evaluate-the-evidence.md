> **In short.** Evaluate evidence for the particular claim it is being used to support. Trace the report to its origin, inspect how observations were selected, and ask what alternatives could produce them. Several reports can depend on one underlying source. A precise measurement can still answer the wrong question. Stronger evidence closes consequential gaps rather than merely adding impressive detail.

## Re-learn

### Trace the support

Imagine three articles reporting that a city's new lighting reduced accidents by 30%. They all repeat one municipal press release. That is three channels of communication, not three independent measurements. Find the original comparison, its dates, the counted accidents, and the population or exposure used in the calculation.

For testimony, relevant expertise and trustworthiness need assessment alongside what was actually asserted and what evidence supports it. Groarke's expert-opinion scheme asks these questions explicitly. The fictional lighting case adds a practical tracing task: identify which report has access to the measurement rather than assuming that repetition establishes independence. [@groarke2026, § 4.4]

An original document is not automatically trustworthy. It may omit failed comparisons, use a changing definition, or present an estimate without its uncertainty. Tracing gives you access to questions that a headline cannot answer.

### Check what the numbers measure

If accidents fell from ten to seven, the reduction is three events and 30% of the original count. Those stipulated numbers do not establish that lighting caused it. Ask whether traffic volume, weather, road works, recording or other safety measures changed at the same time.

The IEP post hoc and common-cause discussions distinguish causal inference from simply observing succession or association. A better comparison needs an account of what would have happened without the lighting, not just a more accurate description of what happened afterward. [@fallacy-iep, Post Hoc, Common Cause]

The relevant outcome may also be accident rates per journey, injury severity, or risk to different road users. A decline in total accidents alongside a larger decline in traffic can differ from a decline in risk per journey. State the denominator and the target before assessing the result.

## Beyond the chapter

### Account for selection

Suppose the city installed lighting at junctions selected for unusually high accident counts. Some reduction might occur because temporary fluctuations do not fully persist. A comparison should reflect that selection. The IEP regression entry identifies the mistake of assigning a regression-related change to an intervention. [@fallacy-iep, Regression]

Suppose instead that ten junctions were changed but only the three largest improvements appear in the report. The missing outcomes affect the assessment even if those three figures are correct. The IEP suppressed-evidence account explains how deliberate or inadvertent omission can distort support. [@fallacy-iep, Suppressed Evidence]

Ask which cases were eligible, which were reported, and why some were unavailable. A missing record is not automatically evidence of deception. It is a limitation whose effect on the inference needs consideration.

### Match evidence to scope

A result from a comparable city can inform a local prediction without fixing it. Road design, traffic patterns and implementation may alter the outcome. A detailed local estimate may still depend on uncertain assumptions. Report those dependencies instead of using the word *study* as a guarantee of generality.

Hitchcock's account of observational abilities distinguishes observation from the inferences attached to it and notes that reliable observation may require specialist knowledge. Knowing which measurements matter is itself part of domain competence. [@hitchcock2024, § 9]

Use [causal inference](deeper:10-science-and-evidence/causation-and-causal-inference) for design assumptions and [finding the crux](deeper:16-critical-thinking-toolkit/step-6-find-the-crux) for deciding which gap deserves the next investigation. Evidence evaluation should end with a stated contribution to the claim, not a score for the document's appearance.
