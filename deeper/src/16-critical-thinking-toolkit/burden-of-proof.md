> **In short.** A burden assigns responsibility for supporting a claim in a particular discussion or procedure. A standard says how much support is required. Failure to meet the burden can justify withholding acceptance without proving the opposite. Presumptions can organise a decision while remaining open to challenge. Make the claim, responsibility and consequence of non-support explicit.

## Re-learn

### Separate three questions

Imagine a committee deciding whether a club qualifies for a grant. The club must submit attendance records; the committee must state which records count; and a rule determines what happens if they are insufficient. Those are questions about who supplies support, the required standard, and the procedural result.

Not awarding the grant under that rule would not establish that the club has no members. It may establish only that eligibility was not demonstrated. Similarly, refusing to accept a factual assertion because its only offered ground is that nobody disproved it leaves the underlying truth unsettled.

The IEP misplaced-burden entry explains the error of demanding disproof instead of supplying support for one's assertion. Its appeal-to-ignorance discussion distinguishes unsupported leaps from reasonable absence inferences when discoverable evidence would be expected. [@fallacy-iep, Misplaced Burden of Proof, Appeal to Ignorance]

### Answer the challenge actually made

If a critic says that the club's list is outdated, they have made a claim about the list that may itself need support. That does not erase the club's responsibility to establish eligibility. Several burdens can coexist because several claims are at issue.

Nor does a request for clarification automatically assert the opposite conclusion. Asking how membership was counted is different from asserting that the count is false. Keep those moves distinct so that inquiry does not become an endless exchange of demands for proof of positions nobody took.

## Beyond the chapter

### Rules serve particular purposes

Hansen's account of pragma-dialectics includes defending a challenged standpoint among the rules of a critical discussion. It also reviews Walton's approach, which distinguishes different dialogue settings. The burden appropriate to a joint inquiry need not mirror a formal grant procedure or a negotiation. [@hansen2024b, § 3.6]

Agreeing to use a presumption can let a procedure reach a result despite uncertainty. It should not be confused with certainty about the presumed proposition. The procedure's purpose and the consequences of mistakes matter to assessing the rule itself.

For a fictional emergency decision about moving equipment away from a suspected leak, waiting for conclusive proof may have a different cost from temporarily moving it. That practical comparison can justify action under uncertainty without establishing that the leak exists. Keep the factual assessment and the decision threshold visible.

### Evidence can alter what needs answering

Once reliable attendance records support eligibility, a bare suggestion that they might be wrong contributes little. A specific discrepancy can matter more. Assess its significance rather than treating every hypothetical possibility as equally weighty.

Conversely, an established practice is not exempt from criticism merely because it is the default. If the criterion itself is disputed, explain the reasons for it. Groarke's treatment of premise acceptability includes the setting and rules governing a dialogue, showing why a procedural premise can need more than an assertion of factual truth. [@groarke2026, § 4.1]

See [the fallacy fallacy](deeper:15-fallacies/the-fallacy-fallacy) for the difference between defeated support and a false conclusion, and [the ethics of discussion](deeper:16-critical-thinking-toolkit/the-ethics-of-discussion) for obligations that make answering possible.
