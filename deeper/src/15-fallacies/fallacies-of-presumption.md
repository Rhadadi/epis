> **In short.** Some arguments fail because a crucial assumption has not been earned. The inference may be valid while a premise excludes real possibilities or assumes the disputed conclusion. Questions can hide assumptions too. Expose the assumption, ask what supports it, and preserve whatever remains useful in the argument. An unsupported claim need not be false, but it cannot do the work of an established premise.

## Re-learn

### A valid form with an unsupported menu

Imagine a library committee deciding whether to replace its catalogue. One member offers only two options: buy the proposed system or abandon reliable records. Rejecting abandonment then seems to establish the purchase. The missing premise is that these exhaust the available options. Maintaining the current system, repairing it, or comparing another supplier may also be possible.

The error lies in that premise, rather than in inferring one alternative after excluding the other. The IEP account of the false dilemma distinguishes an unfairly limited menu from a genuinely exhaustive choice. Two options are not automatically suspicious; their completeness needs support when it matters. [@fallacy-iep, Black-or-White]

### Reasons that return to their starting point

Suppose the proposed catalogue is declared reliable because its records are accurate, and accuracy is defended only by saying that a reliable catalogue produced them. These statements provide no independent route to reliability. Begging the question concerns the support offered for disputed premises; it is not simply the fact that a valid conclusion follows from its premises. The IEP entry discusses independent defence and competing accounts of the distinction. [@fallacy-iep, Begging the Question]

A repair might compare a sample of records with independently identified books and corrections. That evidence can still have limitations, but it gives a doubter something to assess without first accepting the conclusion. See [the problem of the criterion](deeper:06-justification/the-problem-of-the-criterion) for why this local diagnosis does not settle every foundational issue.

### Questions that demand a concession

Asking how long the librarians have concealed errors assumes concealment. Answering with a duration may concede an accusation that has never been established. Separate the questions: were there errors, who knew about them, and was any information intentionally withheld? The IEP account of complex questions identifies the controversial presupposition embedded in their wording. [@fallacy-iep, Complex Question]

## Beyond the chapter

### What a failed search shows

If no error has been found, the catalogue is not thereby infallible. Yet a careful search can provide evidence of reliability. What matters is how likely the search was to detect the kinds of error at issue. The IEP account of appeal to ignorance allows reasonable absence inferences when an existing object or condition would have left discoverable evidence. [@fallacy-iep, Appeal to Ignorance]

In the fictional library, checking twenty easy entries says little about rare-book records that the check never sampled. Checking a representative set with an effective comparison method supports a more substantial conclusion. Neither warrants certainty about every record. Specify the search's scope before claiming what its silence establishes.

### Unstated assumptions are not all fallacies

Every practical argument leaves some background implicit. The useful question is whether a consequential assumption is questionable and needs defence here. Groarke distinguishes acceptable premises from merely true or rhetorically attractive ones, and notes the importance of the dialogue's setting. [@groarke2026, § 4.1]

Selection can also hide an assumption that the evidence presented is complete. If only successful trials of the new catalogue are reported, ask what happened in the others. Incomplete evidence can mislead even when each reported fact is accurate; the IEP discussion treats deliberate suppression and inadvertent omission separately. See [motivated reasoning](deeper:14-psychology-of-reasoning/motivated-reasoning) for why diagnosis need not presume dishonesty. [@fallacy-iep, Suppressed Evidence]
