> **In short.** A reason can be true and still fail to support the claim being disputed. Relevance depends on the conclusion and on how the reason is supposed to bear on it. A speaker's interests may matter to the reliability of testimony without refuting an independently checkable calculation. Emotion can identify what matters without establishing what happened. Diagnose the missing connection before attaching a fallacy label.

## Re-learn

### Fix the target

Consider a fictional meeting about a proposed bridge. An engineer says its supports meet the specified load requirement. An opponent replies that the engineer is unpleasant, the town needs jobs, and most residents favour construction. Those remarks concern different questions: personal conduct, economic benefit, and public preference. None by itself answers whether the supports meet the load requirement.

Change the conclusion to whether the town should build the bridge, and employment and residents' preferences may become relevant. They still need weighing against cost, safety and alternatives. Relevance belongs to a reason's relationship to a particular conclusion, rather than to a permanent list of forbidden topics. Informal logic's acceptability, relevance and sufficiency criteria keep these assessments separate. [@groarke2026, § 4.1]

### Arguments and witnesses

An ad hominem error uses an irrelevant criticism of a person to dismiss their reasoning. The difficult word is *irrelevant*: personal conduct may matter when judging suitability for a role, while having no bearing on an astronomical calculation. The IEP treatment explicitly makes this contextual distinction. [@fallacy-iep, Ad Hominem]

For the bridge, a financial interest is a reason to scrutinise the engineer's testimony. It does not make a displayed arithmetic step false. Ask for the measurements, assumptions and an independent review. If the argument depends entirely on trusting an uncheckable report, credibility has greater importance. If its premises can be established independently, the report's origin does less work.

The same distinction protects legitimate expert testimony. The IEP entry treats much reliance on relevant authorities as reasonable, while warning about unsuitable expertise, misquotation and untrustworthy reporting. Expertise should match the question; a famous actor's endorsement does not supply engineering competence. See [experts and novices](deeper:12-social-epistemology/experts-and-novices). [@fallacy-iep, Appeal to Authority]

## Beyond the chapter

### Feelings and practical reasons

A resident's fear of another accident can be a reason to investigate safety promptly. The seriousness of the possible harm can affect a decision under uncertainty. Neither fact establishes that this bridge will fail. Conversely, a reassuring presentation cannot establish that it is safe. The IEP discussion distinguishes using emotion in argument from using it to replace relevant information; Groarke also discusses legitimate emotional dimensions of argument. [@fallacy-iep, Appeal to Emotions; @groarke2026, § 4.2]

Keep factual and practical conclusions visible. A safety report may support a claim about risk. A further premise about acceptable risk and available alternatives is needed for a recommendation. Someone who questions that further premise has not necessarily rejected the report.

### Restore the disputed claim

A straw man substitutes an easier target for the opponent's position. In the bridge case, opposing this design need not mean opposing every bridge. A red herring directs attention away from the issue. Both defects can be repaired by restating the disputed claim and asking how the response bears on it. The IEP entries explain these distinct failures. [@fallacy-iep, Straw Man, Red Herring]

A useful objection names the connection: the employment premise supports a benefit of construction but does not support the load calculation. That statement tells the other person what remains unanswered. A bare accusation of irrelevance can leave the same gap it criticises.
