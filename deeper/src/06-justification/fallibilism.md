> **In short.** Fallibilism allows adequate justification, and on many accounts knowledge, without a guarantee against error. It does not make a false proposition knowledge. It concerns the limits of our support or belief-forming methods, not a permission to guess. Recognizing those limits can coexist with strong confidence when the evidence warrants it. The central dispute is whether the possibility of error prevents knowledge or only prevents certainty.

## Re-learn

### Supported and still vulnerable

You check a delivery address against the current order, a customer's confirmation, and the labeled parcel. These checks can provide good support. A copying error affecting all three remains possible. Fallibilism permits an epistemic achievement despite that lack of guarantee; it does not pretend the checks exclude every possible failure [@hetherington-iep, §§ 3, 9].

The strength of the support still matters. Three records copied from one erroneous source differ from three independent checks. A fallibilist need not give them equal confidence. Accepting that justification can be less than infallible leaves room for distinctions between weak support, adequate support, and excellent support.

Nor does knowledge become false knowledge. If the address is wrong, the belief is not knowledge of the correct address. A fallibilist claim is that a true belief can qualify as knowledge even though the basis does not make error impossible. Truth remains a condition distinct from the degree of support [@hetherington-iep, § 9].

### A feeling is not a guarantee

Confidence and doubt are psychological attitudes. A person can feel no doubt while overlooking decisive contrary evidence, or feel doubt despite excellent support. Hetherington distinguishes actual doubt from the question whether justification is conclusive. Fallibility cannot be measured merely by asking how certain someone feels [@hetherington-iep, § 3].

The same distinction applies to humility. Saying that you might be wrong can express appropriate caution, but the phrase alone does not improve your evidence. Revisions should respond to reasons. Constantly retreating from a supported answer merely because error is conceivable can be as unhelpful as refusing to revisit it when a real defect appears.

## Beyond the chapter

### Necessary truths and fallible methods

A mathematical truth cannot become false because circumstances change. Nevertheless, a person can reach a correct mathematical answer through a method capable of mistakes: unreliable memory, a careless calculation, or an inadequately checked proof. The IEP entry separates the necessity of the proposition from the fallibility of the process supporting belief in it [@hetherington-iep, § 4].

This prevents another confusion. A valid inference preserves truth if its premises are true; the person can still be mistaken about the premises or about whether the inference is valid. Fallibility in the believer's justification is compatible with an entailment between the contents. Logical relations and a reasoner's access to them are different matters [@hetherington-iep, §§ 3, 4].

### The awkwardness of announcing uncertainty

An assertion that you know something while admitting you might be wrong can sound unstable. One possible explanation is that asserting knowledge ordinarily invites reliance, while the added caveat seems to withdraw that invitation. Whether the discomfort reveals a contradiction or a conversational defect requires argument. The survey compares this issue with Moorean sentences that can describe true situations yet sound inappropriate when asserted [@hetherington-iep, §§ 8, 9].

Thus linguistic oddness should be evidence for a theory, not a substitute for one. Conversely, a philosophical account allowing fallible knowledge must explain ordinary usage rather than dismiss it. The tension helps identify which notion of possibility, which degree of support, and which conversational context the claim involves.
