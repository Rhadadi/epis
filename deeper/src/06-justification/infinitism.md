> **In short.** Infinitism says that an appropriate chain of reasons has no arbitrary endpoint and no question-begging repetition. Further reasons must remain available, but a person need not recite infinitely many reasons at once. The positive account must explain what availability means and why the reasons actually improve support. It is not a license to postpone every answer indefinitely. The main disagreement concerns whether justification needs a final source or can be generated through continued reasoning.

## Re-learn

### Continuing without returning

You defend an estimate with a calculation, the calculation with measurements, and the measurements with information about the procedure. If you are asked about that information, you can offer another relevant reason. An infinitist rejects declaring one reason exempt merely because defending it would be inconvenient. She also rejects returning to the original estimate as the basis for trusting the procedure [@inf-epis-iep, § 1].

Two negative conditions follow: avoid circular support and avoid an arbitrary final reason. They do not jointly constitute a complete positive account. A list of unrelated claims can be endless and nonrepeating without supporting anything. The links need to be appropriate reasons, and their relation to the believer needs explanation [@inf-epis-iep, § 1; @comesana-klein2026, § 6.2].

The ordinary fact that you have uttered finitely many reasons is compatible with different theories. A foundationalist might say support has already reached a non-inferential basis. A coherentist might say the system supplies it. An infinitist might say further reasons remain available. The conversation's length does not decide among them.

### Availability is different from completion

You can possess a capacity you have not exhausted. Being able to continue counting does not mean you have already counted every number. This analogy explains why a finite mind need not have consciously entertained an infinite list. It does not prove that all the required epistemic reasons are available: the relevant capacities and supporting relations still need to be identified [@comesana-klein2026, § 6.2].

Nor does availability mean that an omniscient observer could invent a defense you could never access. An infinitist must say how the reasons belong to your epistemic situation. Without that connection, the proposal would turn a person's arbitrary belief into a justified one whenever some unknown infinite sequence could be imagined.

## Beyond the chapter

### Reasoning as enhancement

The IEP survey distinguishes reason-enhanced justification from a model of pure transmission. On an enhancement account, giving a suitable reason can improve a belief's support even before that reason has itself received a completed defense. This helps explain why an infinitist need not wait for a nonexistent last member before any justification begins [@inf-epis-iep, § 1].

That proposal has costs. It must explain why an unjustified assertion does not enhance support simply by being offered as a reason. It must also state how the amount of enhancement depends on which reasons have been considered and which remain available. Rejecting a far-end source does not remove these positive obligations.

### The arbitrariness objection remains disputed

Infinitists press the foundationalist to explain why a basic reason is justified without another reason. Foundationalists reply that non-inferential support is not arbitrariness. If an experience supplies support, the absence of a further belief does not mean the absence of any support. The disagreement cannot be resolved just by calling every endpoint arbitrary [@inf-epis-iep, § 1; @hasan-fumerton2022, § 1].

Coherentism introduces another complication. Holistic coherentists do not ordinarily endorse the simple question-begging circles infinitists reject. Both views can treat reasoning as generating epistemic value rather than transmitting a fixed quantity from a final source. Their agreement on that point leaves differences about systems and chains [@inf-epis-iep, § 1].
