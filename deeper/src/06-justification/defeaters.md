> **In short.** A defeater reduces or removes a belief's support. A rebutter supplies evidence against the conclusion; an undercutter challenges the connection between the grounds and the conclusion. The distinction prevents criticism of evidence from being mistaken for proof of the opposite claim. A defeater can itself be challenged by further information. Assess the resulting evidence together rather than counting objections as permanent victories.

## Re-learn

### A message about a canceled train

A friend says your train is canceled. Her message gives you a reason to believe it. You then receive a current station announcement saying it is running. That is evidence against the cancellation claim: a rebutter. Alternatively, you discover that your friend checked a notice for a different date. That undermines her message as a basis without establishing that today's train is running [@kelly2016, § 1].

The difference concerns what the new information supports. A rebutter points toward a contrary answer. An undercutter makes the original evidence less informative. After an undercutter, suspension may be appropriate: you still need to find out whether the train is canceled. Saying that an argument fails does not supply an argument for the opposite conclusion.

The distinction also permits mixed cases. Discovering that a source copied an unreliable notice can undercut its testimony, while reading the official timetable supplies contrary evidence. One discovery can contribute to both assessments. Classify its role in the actual inference rather than assuming each consideration must belong to only one category.

### Correcting the objection

Suppose your friend sends a screenshot showing that she did check today's notice, and you discover that the first date display was a formatting error. This challenges the undercutter. It can restore some support without guaranteeing the original claim. The notice might itself be outdated, or contrary testimony might remain. Restoring one connection is not the same as proving the conclusion beyond dispute.

Defeasible reasoning allows additional information to change what is warranted. This differs from classical logical consequence: valid entailment does not cease to be valid merely because new premises arrive. Rather, an evidential rule that normally permits a conclusion can have exceptions. The SEP discussion of nonmonotonic reasoning explains this distinction [@koons2025, § 5.1].

## Beyond the chapter

### The level of the challenge matters

Sometimes the new information concerns your handling of the evidence: you learn that you used the wrong units or overlooked an entry. This can undercut the inference without establishing a particular corrected result. Separate the original observation from the interpretation and the calculation. A defect in one need not erase all the others.

There is also a difference between evidence you actually have and evidence an ideal investigator would have acquired. Whether neglected information defeats present justification depends partly on what theory of justification is being used. Accounts emphasizing possessed evidence distinguish the present evidential question from a separate question about responsible inquiry. The IEP evidentialism entry discusses that distinction directly [@evidentia-iep, § 3.c.ii].

### Defeat can be misleading

A source warning can seem convincing even when the source was in fact accurate. The epistemic role of the warning therefore should not be inferred just from the original conclusion's truth. Kelly distinguishes effects on evidence and justification from whether the world ultimately agrees with the belief. A reasonable revision can move a person away from truth when the new evidence is misleading [@kelly2016, § 1].

In discussion, state the narrower conclusion your objection supports. A study with a defective measurement may fail to establish a treatment effect; that failure does not establish that the treatment has no effect. An independent, better measurement could later support the same conclusion. Distinguishing rebutting and undercutting roles keeps the inquiry open to that possibility.
