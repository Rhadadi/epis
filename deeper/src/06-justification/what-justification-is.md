> **In short.** Justification concerns the epistemic standing of a belief or the support available for believing it. It is different from truth: a reasonable belief can turn out false. Having good evidence also differs from actually believing because of that evidence. Philosophers disagree about whether justification is best explained through evidence, reliability, responsibility, or other conditions. Keeping these questions separate makes an assessment more informative than simply calling a belief rational.

## Re-learn

### A timetable and a lucky guess

You consult the current timetable and believe a bus leaves at six. An unnoticed service change makes that belief false. Another passenger guesses six without checking and happens to be right about a different bus. The first passenger can have support while being wrong; the second can be right without appropriate support. The truth of a conclusion and the quality of its basis are different matters [@steup-neta2025, § 3].

Now imagine that the correct timetable is open in front of you, but you ignore it and decide from a horoscope. The evidence supports a six o'clock departure, and you believe that departure time, yet your actual route to the belief is defective. Propositional justification concerns whether you have sufficient support for a proposition. Doxastic justification concerns whether the belief you hold is appropriately based on support [@ichikawa-steup2026, § 1.3.3].

These are two properties, not rival philosophical schools. A theory can account for both. Their separation helps explain why finding supporting evidence after adopting a conclusion does not automatically show that the conclusion was well-founded when adopted. What matters to the actual belief includes how the evidence now informs or sustains it.

### Having justification and giving a justification

You can have a well-supported perceptual belief without rehearsing a defense. You can also give a polished defense for a poorly supported conclusion. Justification as epistemic standing differs from the activity of justifying something in conversation. A demand for a defense can reveal weaknesses, but a person's ability to give speeches should not silently become the whole standard [@ichikawa-steup2026, § 1.3.1].

Likewise, initial support differs from support after contrary information is considered. A departure board provides a reason; a notice that the board is showing yesterday's services can defeat it. Losing support does not show that there was never any, and possessing an initial reason does not guarantee that the all-things-considered belief is justified [@hasan-fumerton2022, § 4].

## Beyond the chapter

### Blameless, supported, and reliably formed

Someone can be blameless because she lacked a reasonable way to avoid an error. It remains a further question whether her grounds supplied adequate epistemic support. The SEP overview distinguishes deontological accounts, framed through duties and permissions, from accounts emphasizing a suitable likelihood of truth. The disagreement concerns what justification is meant to capture [@steup-neta2025, § 3.1].

A reliable process and fitting evidence can also point in different directions in unusual cases. An externalist can give unnoticed process reliability a role; an internalist can emphasize what the person possesses or can access. Saying that a belief is justified without identifying the intended conception can hide the disagreement [@steup-neta2025, §§ 3.2, 3.3].

### The basing relation needs more than a slogan

Ask whether your supporting considerations influence your belief, but do not treat one counterfactual question as a complete philosophical test. You might retain a belief if one reason disappeared because you have another good reason. Conversely, a reason can causally affect belief through an inappropriate route. Korcz's survey examines why causal, counterfactual, and other accounts of basing need qualifications [@korcz2026, §§ 1, 2].

The useful question is what role the reasons actually play. Are they premises, direct experiential grounds, later corroboration, or decorations attached to a conclusion? That diagnosis is more precise than assuming that any supporting fact justifies any matching belief.
