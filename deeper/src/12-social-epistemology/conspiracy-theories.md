> **In short.** A conspiracy explanation attributes an event to people coordinating secretly. That description alone does not establish that the explanation is false. A responsible assessment compares its evidence, assumptions, and explanatory performance with alternatives. Secrecy can explain why some evidence is difficult to obtain, but it does not automatically support every accusation. The task is to investigate a specific claim without either presuming institutions infallible or treating all contrary evidence as part of the plot.

## Re-learn

### Specify what is alleged

Here is an invented case. A contractor suspects two bidders coordinated their prices before a public tender. The allegation identifies agents, a shared goal, and concealed coordination. Documents, communication records, or an independently established pattern could provide relevant evidence. The mere fact that the prices are similar leaves several explanations open.

Now expand the claim: everyone who investigates the tender is secretly involved, and any contradictory record proves the cover-up. This version adds assumptions without showing what evidence could distinguish it from the alternatives. The problem is how the explanation handles information, rather than the mere presence of secrecy.

An unexplained detail in the official account deserves attention. It does not by itself establish the proposed conspiracy. Compare what each account explains and what remains difficult for it. An alternative with its own unexplained details cannot gain unlimited support merely by pointing out one anomaly elsewhere.

### Investigate distrust too

If the tender authority has a documented conflict of interest, that matters. It can motivate independent scrutiny. It does not establish that every employee colluded or every record was altered. Keep the scope of justified distrust tied to the evidence.

The same standard applies to the accuser. A financial or political interest warrants examination of their report, rather than automatic dismissal. A motive to make an accusation and evidence that the accusation is false are different considerations.

## Beyond the chapter

### Neutral and loaded definitions

Pauly surveys definitions ranging from epistemically neutral accounts of secret coordination to descriptions that build an epistemic defect into the term. Philosophers commonly use a relatively minimal definition to keep evaluation open. Moving unnoticed between these meanings creates a shortcut: a claim is labeled a conspiracy theory and then dismissed by the negative associations of the label. [@conspiracy-theories-iep, § 2].

An official explanation can itself invoke a conspiracy. Conversely, opposing an official account need not imply secret coordination. State the content of the allegation rather than letting its relationship to authority determine its classification or credibility.

### Secrecy complicates simple tests

The entry describes disagreements about falsifiability, missing evidence, and errant data. Some authors emphasize that concealment makes evidence difficult to obtain; others stress that unsuccessful searches can still reduce credibility under appropriate comparisons. There is no universal rule that the absence of a record either refutes or confirms the allegation. Ask whether a record would probably exist and be discoverable if the proposed explanation were true. [@conspiracy-theories-iep, § 4].

Likewise, a large alleged conspiracy introduces questions about coordination and secrecy. Pauly presents both scale arguments and criticisms concerning hierarchies, control, and the actual number of involved people. Treating an entire institution as knowingly complicit can inflate that number without evidence. [@conspiracy-theories-iep, § 4].

The chapter's warning about self-sealing explanations is useful when it identifies a specific maneuver that shields a claim from scrutiny. It should not become a rule that all secret activity must be easy to falsify. Investigate positive support, alternatives, and the handling of counterevidence together.
