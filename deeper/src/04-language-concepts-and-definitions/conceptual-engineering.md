> **In short.** Conceptual engineering asks not what our concepts are but what they should be. The idea is older than the name: Carnap called it explication, and saw philosophy itself as "a kind of conceptual engineering" for the language of science. An explication keeps what matters in an old concept and openly lets the rest go. Today's ameliorative projects ask the same question about social concepts, such as gender and race, in light of goals like equality.

## Re-learn

### Step by step

#### 1. Carnap's explication

"An explication aims to respect some central uses of a term but is stipulative on others." It may be offered "as an absolute improvement of an existing, imperfect concept", or as "a “good thing to mean” by the term in a specific context for a particular purpose" [@gupta-mackereth2023, § 1.5].

#### 2. Function over meaning

The set-theoretic definition of an ordered pair has consequences the ordinary notion lacks. "But the mismatch is not an objection to the explication. What is important for explication is not antecedent meaning but function. So long as the latter is preserved, the former can be let go" [@gupta-mackereth2023, § 1.5].

#### 3. Philosophy as engineering

For the logical empiricists, concepts were tools, "none of which was uniquely correct", so choosing among them was "a practical decision about usefulness for certain purposes". Philosophy can analyse existing concepts, make vague ones precise through explication, and investigate wholly new ones. "It becomes, thus, a kind of conceptual engineering" [@creath2026, § 4.1].

### Common confusions

- **Conceptual engineering is a recent invention.** Creath notes that "Conceptual analysis, explication, construction, and engineering continue to be fruitful ideas in philosophy, though it is not always understood how much of this was initiated or shaped by Carnap and other logical empiricists" [@creath2026, § 4.1].

## Beyond the chapter

### What the chapter leaves out

- **Why it grew.** The critique of conceptual analysis "has also generated proposals for various types of normative, revisionary projects". "Instead of asking which cases are or are not covered by a concept, they ask how a concept should be modified or what new concept should be adopted in its place, given the practical context in which the concept is used—for example, given the goal of promoting social equality (Haslanger 2012, Cappelen 2018)" [@margolis-laurence2023, § 5.2].
- **Two concepts instead of one.** Sometimes engineering means splitting a concept. Carnap concluded that the frequency and confirmation approaches to probability explicate "two different concepts of probability", both useful [@creath2026, § 4.4]. See [Verbal disputes](deeper:04-language-concepts-and-definitions/verbal-disputes).
- **The logician's conditional.** The truth-functional "if" is an engineered concept: it "differs from the ordinary conditional in some essential respects", but it "can be put forward as an explication of the ordinary conditional for certain purposes in certain contexts" [@gupta-mackereth2023, § 1.5]. See [Propositional logic](deeper:03-logic-and-arguments/propositional-logic).

### What the chapter simplifies

- **Transparency as the test.** The chapter distinguishes legitimate engineering from persuasive definition by transparency. The explication literature adds a second test: whether the new concept preserves the function that made the old one worth having [@gupta-mackereth2023, § 1.5]. A transparent proposal that loses the function is still a bad one.
