> **In short.** Reflective equilibrium is the method of adjusting principles and judgments about cases against each other until they fit. Rawls named it; Goodman described the same procedure for rules of inference. Its central questions are which judgments to start from and whether a coherent system built from biased judgments is just a tidy prejudice. Recent evidence that even professional philosophers' judgments shift with irrelevant order and framing makes that worry concrete.

## Re-learn

### Step by step

#### 1. Why a method is needed

"What we actually accept is fraught with idiosyncrasy and vulnerable to vagaries of history and personality" (Elgin), or shows "irregularities and distortions" (Rawls). "John Rawls proposed to address these concerns through the method of reflective equilibrium" [@knight2025, preamble].

#### 2. Considered judgments

The starting points are "considered judgments": "those judgments in which our moral capacities are most likely to be displayed without distortion" [@knight2025, § 1.1]. Rawls proposes that "we can discard those judgments made with hesitation, or in which we have little confidence" [@knight2025, § 1.2].

#### 3. Wide equilibrium

"On the standard wide reflective equilibrium, we are to consider all possible descriptions to which one might plausibly conform one's judgments together with all relevant philosophical arguments for them" [@knight2025, preamble].

### Common confusions

- **The method first checks which judgments are justified.** Friends of reflective equilibrium "largely reject" an epistemic filter on starting judgments, because "it makes epistemological determinations in advance of the reflective process where these are rather the proper upshot of that process" [@knight2025, § 1.3].

## Beyond the chapter

### What the chapter leaves out

- **Dropping unconfident judgments may hurt.** Excluding low-confidence judgments "may increase rather than decrease the distortion" the method is meant to correct. Knight's example is someone with far-right commitments who feels some unconfident pull toward equality and liberty: excluding those judgments would remove what might have found principled support [@knight2025, § 1.2].
- **Order and framing.** "One study found that professional philosophers were vulnerable to order and framing effects, and that this was not reduced by reflection or expertise" (Schwitzgebel and Cushman 2015) [@knight2025, § 3.4]. Singer argues that judgments about the footbridge trolley case are "emotionally-driven evolved responses to circumstances that no longer obtain" [@knight2025, § 3.4].

### What the chapter simplifies

- **Goodman and Rawls.** The chapter quotes Goodman on rules of inference. The method developed most fully in moral and political philosophy, by Rawls and Norman Daniels, "with some reference to versions of the method developed in other branches of philosophy (e.g., Goodman 1965" [@knight2025, preamble].
