> **In short.** You use a word to talk about the world; you mention it to talk about the word. Quotation marks are the usual signal. The distinction is drilled into every logic student, and its practical point is simple: a fact about a label is not a fact about the thing labelled. But exactly how to draw the line turns out to be hard, and quotation itself is one of the trickier devices in language.

## Re-learn

### Step by step

#### 1. The basic contrast

Compare "Jim went to Paris" with "‘Jim’ has three letters". In the first, the word "is used to talk about (or signify, or denote) a person"; in the second, it is used "to talk about (or signify or denote) a word". "In (D1), ‘Jim’ is being used and in (D2) it is being mentioned" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 2.2].

#### 2. Why it matters

When language is used to talk about language, "a linguistic device is needed that ‘turns language on itself’". Quotation is "our primary meta-linguistic tool" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, preamble]. Without it you cannot say that "‘Snow is white’ is true in English iff snow is white", or that "‘Bachelor’ has eight letters" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, preamble].

#### 3. Quotation blocks substitution

Quotation is "a paradigmatic opaque context, i.e. a context in which substitution of synonymous or co-referential expressions can fail to preserve truth-value" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, preamble]. "Bachelor" and "unmarried man" mean the same, but "‘Bachelor’ has eight letters" is true and "‘Unmarried man’ has eight letters" is false. This is the same feature that makes belief contexts tricky in [Sense and reference](04-language-concepts-and-definitions.md#sense-and-reference).

### Common confusions

- **Quotation marks always mark mention.** Spoken language has "no obvious correlates of quotation marks", and in writing italics, bold face or indentation can do the job instead [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 1.2]. "My name is Donald" mentions a name with no marks at all [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 1.2].
- **Quoting someone is pure mention.** In "Quine said that quotation ‘has a certain anomalous feature’", the quoted words are both used, to say what Quine claimed, and mentioned, to report that he used those words [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 4]. This "mixed quotation" is everywhere in journalism: "open or scroll to a newspaper and read until you find the first mixed quotation (it shouldn't take long)" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 4].

## Beyond the chapter

### What the chapter leaves out

- **The line is harder to draw than it looks.** Davidson recalled that quotation was introduced to him "as a somewhat shady device" with "a stern sermon on the sin of confusing the use and mention of expressions" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, preamble]. Yet the natural refinements fail. "Mentioned means quoted" fails because on some theories an expression can be mentioned without being quoted. "Mentioned means used to refer to itself" makes mention a kind of use, and it is controversial whether quotations are referring expressions at all [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 2.2].
- **Semantic ascent.** Carnap distinguished the material mode ("one is a number") from the formal mode ("‘one’ is a number word"). Recasting a claim as a claim about language "may succeed in removing some of its metaphysical mystery" [@cappelen-mckeever2024, § 7]. That is one source of the "linguistic turn" mentioned in [Why language is an epistemic issue](04-language-concepts-and-definitions.md#why-language-is-an-epistemic-issue).
