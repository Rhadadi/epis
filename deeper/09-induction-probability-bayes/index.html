Deeper study · Chapter 9

Induction, Probability, and Bayesian Reasoning

For when a section didn't click, or left you wanting more: each page explains the idea again, tells the full story, fills in what the chapter leaves out, and gives the sources.

Claude Monet, Impression, Sunrise, 1872

← Back to Chapter 9

  • Short study · 3 minKinds of non-deductive inferenceEvidence can favor a conclusion without guaranteeing it. Sampling, applying a population rate, comparing similar cases, and explaining an observation favor conclusions in different ways.
  • Full study · 11 minHume’s problem of inductionBread has always nourished you, so you expect the next loaf to nourish you too. Hume asked what reasoning takes you from the first fact to the second, and argued that none can.
  • Full study · 11 minResponses to HumeNearly three centuries of replies to Hume can be sorted by what they attack. Some look for an a priori argument after all: Kant’s synthetic a priori, inference to the best explanation, Laplace’s probability calculus.
  • Full study · 11 minGoodman’s new riddle of inductionAn observation supports a prediction only after we decide which features belong together. Goodman makes that choice visible by inventing a category that fits every observation so far but gives a different prediction next.
  • Short study · 3 minThe paradox of the ravensAll ravens are black says the same thing as all non-black objects are non-ravens. A green apple is an instance of the second statement.
  • Short study · 3 minProbability: the basicsProbability describes how an assessment is distributed over possibilities. Its rules govern relationships between assessments, while its interpretation says what the numbers mean.
  • Full study · 11 minBayes’ theoremBayes’ theorem connects the chance of evidence given a hypothesis with the chance of the hypothesis given that evidence. Those are different quantities because the evidence may have other causes.
  • Short study · 3 minBayesian epistemologyBayesian epistemology studies rational degrees of confidence and their revision. Its familiar core asks for probabilistic coherence at a time and conditionalization when certain new evidence is learned.
  • Short study · 3 minFull belief and degrees of beliefBelieving a claim and assigning it high confidence are different ways of representing an attitude. A threshold proposal tries to connect them: believe a claim when its probability is high enough.
  • Short study · 3 minCommon errors in probabilistic reasoningProbability mistakes often begin with a changed comparison group. A result’s reliability is confused with the probability of its explanation, or a detailed story is treated as more probable than one of its parts.
  • Short study · 3 minCalibration and scoring rulesA probabilistic forecast should be assessed across outcomes rather than branded wrong whenever a less probable result occurs. Calibration compares stated probabilities with outcome frequencies in groups of predictions.
  • Short study · 3 minStatistics and significanceStatistical significance evaluates data through a specified testing procedure. A p-value is not the probability that the tested hypothesis is true or that the finding will replicate.
