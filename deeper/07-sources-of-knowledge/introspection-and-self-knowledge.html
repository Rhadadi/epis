Introspection and self-knowledge

In short

Self-knowledge names knowledge of oneself; introspection names one proposed route to knowing one’s mental life. Observing your behaviour can yield self-knowledge without being introspection. Even introspective knowledge need not concern every mental state or be immune to every mistake. Some accounts explain self-knowledge through detecting a state; others emphasize thinking about the world or taking responsibility for an attitude. A useful theory specifies both its target and its method.

Layer 1Re-learn

Knowing your mind in different ways

You notice that you keep avoiding a meeting and infer that you are anxious about it. You inspect your expression in a mirror and conclude that you are angry. You also feel a pain without inspecting behaviour or an image. All three can inform you about yourself; they do not obviously use the same route.

Schwitzgebel describes introspection as learning about one’s current or very recent mental states in a distinctive first-person way. Learning about oneself through behaviour, psychology texts, or brain measurements does not ordinarily count as introspection. Beyond that broad contrast, there is no universally accepted account of the required process.

The topic of self-knowledge is broader still. Contemporary discussions usually focus on one’s mental states rather than every fact about one’s identity. Knowledge of an enduring self is a related but separate question. Knowing your current belief does not automatically answer what makes you the same person over time.

Security is not completeness

To call some judgment infallible is to say it cannot be mistaken. To call a person omniscient about their mental life is to say no relevant state escapes their knowledge. These are different claims. A theory could allow highly secure knowledge of a current pain while denying exhaustive awareness of motives, dispositions, or unconscious attitudes.

Even defenses of infallibility often restrict their scope to particular kinds of judgment. The introspection survey discusses restrictions to thoughts about thoughts, phenomenal judgments, or beliefs understood as commitments. It does not present one unrestricted guarantee covering every self-description.

Sources for this part: Schwitzgebel 2024, preamble; Schwitzgebel 2024, § 1.1; Gertler 2024, preamble; Gertler 2024, § 1.1; Schwitzgebel 2024, § 4.1.1.

Layer 3Beyond the chapter

Looking inward is not the only proposal

Self-detection accounts model self-knowledge through identifying an existing mental state. But the literature contains multiple approaches, and some are not readily described as introspective. The alternatives need not be exclusive: different states or circumstances may permit different routes1.

Transparency accounts consider a striking alternative. To answer whether you believe a proposition, you may consider the proposition itself. Asked whether you believe the road is closed, you think about evidence concerning the road, rather than search for an internal object labelled belief2.

That procedure faces a temporal question. Considering the road may create a new belief instead of revealing what you believed before being asked. If the question concerns a previous attitude, a method that settles the current issue may answer something different. Transparency theorists offer responses, so this is an objection to assess rather than a refutation by definition3.

Agentialist accounts emphasize responsibility for one’s attitudes. Gertler explains that Moran grounds an entitlement to the transparency method in that normative relation, rather than just in the method’s reliability. This differs from an empiricist account even when the outward procedure looks similar4.

Respecting reports while assessing explanations

Someone’s report that they feel pain has a different target from their explanation of its medical cause. Similarly, knowing that you resent a decision is distinct from knowing exactly why. Distinguishing the targets helps preserve first-person evidence without treating every causal interpretation as privileged.

This distinction also matters when considering knowledge by presence. Awareness, a conceptual description of that awareness, and a public report can have different conditions. An argument securing one relation should not silently be extended to all three.

Layer 4Sources

Where to go next

Begin with Schwitzgebel’s definition problem and overview of competing processes. Gertler then separates the proposed distinctive features of self-knowledge and explains transparency and agency. Read the restricted infallibility proposals alongside the general definitions; their limits are part of the proposal, not details to discard5.
