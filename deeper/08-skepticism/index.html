Skepticism and Its Answers

For when a section didn't click, or left you wanting more: each page explains the idea again, tells the full story, fills in what the chapter leaves out, and gives the sources.

Caravaggio, The Incredulity of Saint Thomas, c. 1601–02

← Back to Chapter 8

  • Short study · 3 minWhy study skepticism?Skeptical arguments reveal requirements we may be assuming when we claim knowledge. Their value does not depend on accepting every skeptical conclusion.
  • Short study · 3 minVarieties of skepticismSkeptical positions differ in target, strength, and attitude. Doubting knowledge in one domain differs from denying it everywhere.
  • Full study · 10 minPyrrhonian skepticismPyrrhonian skepticism is a practice of setting opposing considerations against one another until judgment is suspended. Sextus presents it as an ability, rather than the doctrine that knowledge is impossible.
  • Short study · 3 minCartesian skepticismDescartes uses doubt to search for a secure foundation. That purpose differs from endorsing doubt as the final outcome of inquiry.
  • Short study · 3 minThe brain in a vatA brain-in-a-vat scenario tests whether matching experiences could conceal a radically different situation. It is a constructed possibility, not a discovered explanation of your current life.
  • Full study · 11 minThe closure argumentClosure says that appropriate deduction can extend knowledge to an implication of what is already known. It does not say that a person automatically knows every logical consequence.
  • Full study · 11 minResponses to skepticismResponses to skepticism disagree about what knowledge requires, what evidence includes, and what an argument must accomplish. Some preserve ordinary knowledge by rejecting the skeptic’s account of error; others restrict closure or explain changes in knowledge language.
  • Short study · 3 minSkepticism about induction, other minds, and the pastFuture events, other people’s minds, and the past present different epistemic challenges. They need not share one skeptical argument or one solution.
  • Short study · 3 minThe simulation argumentBostrom’s simulation argument connects assumptions about conscious simulations with the proportions of different kinds of observers. It concludes a disjunction, not simply that we are simulated.
  • Short study · 3 minHealthy and corrosive skepticismA useful doubt identifies what may be wrong and what would help decide it. A general possibility of error does not discriminate between two competing claims.
