Understanding and wisdom

Going deeper on a section of “What Is Knowledge?”.

In short

Knowing an answer, grasping an explanation and judging how to live well are different achievements. Understanding concerns connections and dependencies, while wisdom includes a wider assessment of belief and conduct. Neither is simply a large collection of facts. Their relations to truth and luck remain disputed. A useful comparison keeps separate the content grasped, the route to that grasp and what the person can do with it.

Layer 1Re-learn

An answer and a connection

Someone might correctly report that a circuit failed without understanding why it failed. Another person might grasp how the short circuit caused the shutdown and how changing the wiring would alter the result. The SEP discussion distinguishes views that locate the object of understanding in relationships among representations from views that locate it in mind-independent relationships. The explanatory connection is not automatically the same thing as a connection among one’s sentences.

That distinction matters when an internally coherent explanation is wrong. Every part of a story can fit every other part without matching the mechanism. Conversely, a diagram may capture an important dependence even if its labels simplify the actual system. Evaluating understanding involves both what is grasped and whether the representation connects appropriately to its target.

Why idealizations are a puzzle

A frictionless plane or ideal gas does not literally match the whole world. Yet a model using one can illuminate a dependence. Some philosophers take this to show that understanding can be nonfactive; others seek to identify what is true in the model’s explanatory contribution. The SEP presents Elgin’s exemplification account, Potochnik’s emphasis on selected causal patterns, and Strevens’s difference-making approach as distinct proposals.

It does not follow that any useful fiction gives understanding. A simplified representation must select something that matters and handle its departures from reality appropriately. The dispute concerns what truth constraints apply, not whether a convincing explanation can freely invent the mechanism.

Wisdom includes assessment of oneself

Ryan’s discussion distinguishes humility, accuracy, knowledge, hybrid and rationality approaches to wisdom. Simple humility is insufficient: someone who correctly believes that they are not wise is not thereby wise. Conversely, appropriate confidence in one’s actual expertise is not automatically arrogance.

The important boundary is between confidence within an established area and confidence beyond it. In Ryan’s discussion of Socrates, the craftsmen’s problem is not possessing craft knowledge; it is extending their claims beyond that expertise. Wisdom can therefore involve calibrating self-assessment rather than denying every capacity one has.

Sources for this part: Grimm 2025, § 2.1; Grimm 2025, § 4.2; Ryan 2020, §§ 1–2; Ryan 2020, § 1.

Layer 3Beyond the chapter

The route to understanding can matter

Even if someone grasps the right cause, it remains possible to ask whether they reached it by careful inquiry or coincidence. The SEP explicitly raises that normative question. It should be separated from whether the content is accurate: two people may endorse the same explanation while having different epistemic grounds1.

This extends the Gettier problem without assuming its verdict transfers unchanged. Some accounts allow forms of luck in understanding that they reject in knowledge. Others resist the separation. The question is how explanatory grasp differs from possession of a true answer2.

Rationality without perfection

Ryan’s deep rationality proposal combines broad justified beliefs, rational appreciation of practical and emotional matters, a life reflecting those beliefs, and restraint about unsupported belief. She presents it as accommodating degrees of wisdom without requiring perfection. The proposal is one account, rather than an uncontested definition3.

An expert who can explain a mechanism may still judge badly about whether to use it. A wise decision-maker may need to acknowledge a technical gap and consult another person. These distinctions connect understanding with Experts and novices and wisdom with Epistemic humility.

Layer 4Sources

Where to go next

Grimm’s Understanding §§ 2.1 and 2.3 distinguish the objects and normative conditions of understanding; § 4.2 treats idealization. Ryan’s Wisdom contrasts the theories rather than treating humility as a slogan. The Value of Knowledge § 4 explains why understanding entered the debate about distinctive epistemic value4.
