Intellectual Virtue and the Ethics of Belief

  • Full study · 13 minThe ethics of belief: Clifford and JamesClifford asks what we owe others when forming beliefs that can shape action and inquiry. James asks whether demanding evidence first can sometimes prevent access to a valuable truth.
  • Short study · 3 minPractical reasons for beliefEvidence bears on whether a claim is true. A practical consideration bears on what believing, accepting, or acting would do for someone.
  • Full study · 13 minVirtue epistemologyVirtue epistemology begins with the capacities and character of knowers. Some theories explain knowledge through reliable competences; others study traits that make inquiry go well.
  • Short study · 3 minA catalogue of intellectual virtuesIntellectual virtues concern how someone inquires, judges, and responds to reasons. A list of virtues becomes useful when it identifies different difficulties in that work.
  • Short study · 3 minIntellectual vicesAn intellectual vice obstructs good inquiry or the handling of knowledge. It can involve a character trait, an attitude, or a way of thinking.
  • Short study · 3 minEpistemic humility and confidenceEpistemic humility concerns how someone handles intellectual limitations. It need not require low confidence in every conclusion.
  • Short study · 3 minOpen-mindedness and its limitsOpen-minded inquiry makes relevant alternatives available for fair consideration. It does not require giving every proposal equal credibility or endless attention.
  • Short study · 3 minPragmatic encroachmentPragmatic encroachment says practical circumstances can partly determine whether someone knows. Its controversial claim holds even when the truth-relevant evidence is unchanged.
  • Short study · 3 minMoral encroachmentMoral considerations can enter the assessment of belief in several different ways. A belief might wrong someone, an inquiry might use wrongful methods, or moral stakes might change what counts as adequate epistemic support.
  • Full study · 13 minFaith, reason, and religious epistemologyReligious epistemology asks what supports religious belief and how it should respond to doubt and disagreement. Faith may involve belief, trust, commitment, hope, or several of these together.
