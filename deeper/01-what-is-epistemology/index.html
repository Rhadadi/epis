What Is Epistemology?

  • Short study · 3 minWhy epistemology matters for critical thinkingCritical thinking asks “Is this true? How do they know? Should I trust this source?” Those are epistemology’s questions, asked quickly.
  • Short study · 3 minThe central questionsEpistemology’s many topics come back to a few questions: What is knowledge? What makes a belief justified?
  • Full study · 15 minThree kinds of knowingEnglish uses one verb, “know”, for at least three different achievements: knowing that something is the case (a fact), knowing how to do something (a skill), and knowing a person, place or experience by acquaintance (direct familiarity). The chapter treats them as three kinds.
  • Full study · 12 minBelief, credence, and acceptanceThree different attitudes can all look like “thinking something is true”. Belief is taking it to be true: all-or-nothing, a settled state that guides what you say and do.
  • Short study · 4 minThe basic vocabularyThe chapter defines the working vocabulary of the field: proposition, truth, evidence, justification, rationality, warrant, certainty, fallibilism, defeater and reasons. Each word has a plain core that the chapter gets right, and each hides a debate.
  • Full study · 18 minThree great distinctionsThe chapter introduces three pairs of words that sound alike but answer three different questions about a statement. A priori / a posteriori asks how you could come to know it: by thinking, or by looking at the world.
  • Full study · 15 minEpistemic reasons versus practical reasonsA reason to believe something can be of two kinds. An epistemic reason bears on whether the belief is true: evidence, argument, reliable testimony.
  • Short study · 4 minEpistemology is normativePsychology describes how people do reason; epistemology asks how they ought to: what they are entitled to believe, and what counts as good evidence. That is what “normative” means.
  • Short study · 3 minThe map of the fieldContemporary epistemology has many branches: traditional, formal, social, virtue, naturalized, feminist, and the epistemology of science, morality and religion. The chapter’s diagram draws them as a tree.
  • Short study · 4 minHow the pieces connect: one news story, twelve questionsThe chapter ends with a headline, “New study: People who drink coffee live longer”, and twelve questions a trained reader would ask about it. Each question belongs to a part of epistemology: testimony, causation, statistics, replication, motivated reasoning, the ethics of belief.
