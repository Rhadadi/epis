## A claim and a reason

Ava's friend says, "That soup is yucky."

That is a **claim**. A claim is something a person says is true.

Ava asks the magic question: **"What makes you say that?"**

"Well… it has carrots in it. I never tried it."

Hmm. Did he taste the soup? No. His reason is not a good one yet.

## About the thing, or about me?

The fox said, "The grapes are sour." That was his claim.

Then Little Grape asked, "What makes you say that?"

The fox said the grapes were high. He said he was tired. Those reasons are about *the fox*. They do not tell us how the grapes taste.

A good reason is about the grapes. "I tasted one and it was sour." "A bird spat one out." "They are small and green, and green grapes are often sour."

## Excuses

Sometimes we want something and can't have it. Then we say, "I didn't want it anyway!"

That is an **excuse**. It sounds like a reason, but it does not show the claim is true. A feeling can explain why we say it. It cannot show that it is true.

Everybody does it. The clever part is to notice it, and ask: **"Is this a reason about the thing, or about me?"**

::: think
- What was the fox's claim? What were his reasons?
- How could the fox have found out if the grapes were sour?
- Can you think of a time you said "I didn't want it anyway"?
:::
