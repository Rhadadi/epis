## Goals

By the end of this unit, children can say:

- **Explorers (7–10):** "I can tell knowing from guessing and hoping." "I can ask *How do you know?*" "I can say why some clues are better than others."
- **Investigators (11–14):** "I can explain why knowledge needs a true belief *and* a good reason." "I can tell a test that a wrong answer would fail from one that anyone could pass." "I can explain why a lucky guess isn't knowledge."

## The idea in adult words

Epistemology asks what knowledge is and how we get it. The traditional starting point treats knowledge as justified true belief; Gettier-style cases such as Russell's stopped clock (a true belief, held for a normally good reason, but true only by luck) show that something more is needed: the reason must connect the belief to the truth. The story makes the same point with evidence: the voice and the paw are tests a wolf can pass, so passing them is weak evidence; the secret song is a test only the right answer passes. See the grown-up guide, [chapter 1](01-what-is-epistemology.md) and [chapter 5, the Gettier problem](05-the-nature-of-knowledge.md#the-gettier-problem).

## The misconception this unit targets

"If I turned out to be right, I knew." Children (and adults) often judge a belief by whether it came out true. The unit separates *being right* from *having a good reason*, and good reasons from reasons that are easy to fake.

## Running the discussion

1. Read or listen to the story together, and stop after the third knock: "Should they open the door? How could they be sure?"
2. Use the "Think about it" questions. Wait at least five seconds after each question. Follow-up moves: "What makes you say that?" "Can you give an example?" "Does anyone see it differently?"
3. Play the two sorting games. The explanations matter more than the score; ask children to argue for the cards they missed.
4. Investigators: work through the stopped-clock puzzle in pairs before reading the answer.
5. Close with the big idea in the children's own words.

## Timing and materials

- Explorers: two sessions of 20–25 minutes (story and talk; games and quick check).
- Investigators: one 45-minute class period.
- Optional: a box with a hidden object for "guess or know?"; a printed set of the sorting cards.

## Easier and harder

- **Easier:** play "guess what's in the bag" first; let children feel the difference between guessing and looking.
- **Harder:** ask Investigators to design a "door test" for a real situation (a phone password, a reply to a message from a stranger) and explain how a faker would try to pass it.
