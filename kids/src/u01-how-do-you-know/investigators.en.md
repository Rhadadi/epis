## Three clues, three strengths

The kids in the story did something smart: they didn't just trust the visitor's words. They asked for **evidence**. But not all evidence is equally strong.

- **The voice.** A rough voice was good evidence that the visitor was *not* Mother. A soft voice was weak evidence that it *was* her, because a wolf can soften his voice with honey.
- **The foot.** The same again. A black foot ruled the wolf out. A white foot proved very little, because flour is easy to find.
- **The song.** This was the strongest test. Only Mother knew the song, so only Mother could pass it.

Notice the pattern. A good test is one that the wrong answer would *fail*. After a trip to the bakery, the wolf could pass the voice test and the foot test. He could never pass the song test.

Detectives, doctors and scientists all look for tests like this. A test that everybody passes, whether they are right or wrong, can't tell you much.

## Being right isn't the same as knowing

Here's a puzzle. Grandma Rose's kitchen clock stopped at exactly three o'clock, but nobody noticed. One afternoon, Sam glances at it, and it happens to be exactly three o'clock. He believes it's three, and he's right.

Does Sam *know* the time?

Most people say no. His belief is true, but only by luck. The clock would have said "three" whatever the time was. The philosopher Bertrand Russell described a clock like this in 1948, and in 1963 Edmund Gettier made puzzles of this kind famous. They show that knowing needs more than being right. It also needs a good reason: one that connects your belief to the truth.

So a **lucky guess** is not knowledge, even when it turns out to be right. And Little Grape's knowledge was not luck. Her test was built so that a wolf would fail it.

## Four ways of holding an idea

| | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Know** | right, for a good reason | "There are seven days in a week." |
| **Believe** | think it's true, for some reason | "The library opens at nine: the sign says so." |
| **Guess** | an answer with little or no reason | "The answer is C." |
| **Hope** | want it to be true | "I hope my team wins." |

Beliefs can be strong or weak, depending on the reasons behind them. The sign on the library door is a good reason, but signs can be out of date. A belief with a strong reason counts as knowledge only if it is also true.

## The detective questions

Every quest on this site uses four questions:

1. What exactly is being said?
2. **How do they know?**
3. **What else could explain it?**
4. How sure should I be?

At the door, the kids asked question 2: *how do we know this is Mother?* Little Grape also asked question 3: *what else could explain a soft voice and a white foot?* A wolf with honey and flour!
