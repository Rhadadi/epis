  2. Jingle, Jangle and Little Grape

    Grandma Rose Here is a very old story. Children in Iran have heard it for a long, long time.

    Once upon a time, a mother goat lived in a little house near the woods. She had three kids. Their names were Jingle, Jangle and Little Grape.

  3. One morning, Mother Goat had to go and find grass.

    Mother Goat Lock the door when I go. Open it only for me. When I come back, I will sing our secret song.

    Mother Goat And remember: I have a soft voice and white feet. The wolf has a rough voice and black feet.

    The kids We will remember!

  4. Soon there was a knock at the door. Knock, knock, knock!

    The kids Who is it?

    Wolf Open the door, my dears. It's your mother!

    The voice was rough and loud. Jingle looked at Jangle.

    Jingle You are not our mother. Our mother has a soft voice. You are the wolf!

  5. The wolf ran off. He ate a big spoon of honey to make his voice sweet. Then he came back.

    Wolf Open the door, my dears. It's your mother!

    Now the voice was soft and sweet. Jangle was not sure. "Show us your foot," he said. A big black foot came under the door.

    Jangle You are not our mother. Our mother has white feet. You are the wolf!

  6. The wolf ran to the baker. He rubbed his feet in white flour. Then he came back again.

    Wolf Open the door, my dears. It's your mother! Look at my white feet.

    The voice was soft. The foot was white. Jingle and Jangle ran to open the door.

  7. Little Grape Wait! A wolf can make his voice soft. A wolf can make his feet white. But only Mother knows our secret song. Sing it, please!

    The wolf did not know the song. He hummed. He coughed. He sang "la la la".

  8. The kids That is not our song. You are the wolf! Go away!

    The wolf was so cross that he ran far into the woods, and he never came back.

  9. At last, Mother Goat came home. She sang the secret song, every word. The kids opened the door and gave her a big hug.

    Mother Goat How did you know it was really me?

    Little Grape A voice can be copied. A white foot can be faked. But nobody else knows our song!

    Grandma Rose In some old books the wolf is very scary. In our story, he just goes away. Now, Ava, Sam: how did Little Grape know?

  10. Three clues, three strengths

    The kids in the story did something smart: they didn't just trust the visitor's words. They asked for evidence. But not all evidence is equally strong.

  11. The voice. A rough voice was good evidence that the visitor was not Mother. A soft voice was weak evidence that it was her, because a wolf can soften his voice with honey.

  12. The foot. A black foot gave the wolf away. A white foot proved very little, because flour is easy to find.

  13. The song. This was the strongest test. Only Mother knew the song, so only Mother could pass it.

  14. A good test is one that the wrong answer would fail.

    After a trip to the bakery, the wolf could pass two of the tests. Which test was hardest for him to pass?

    • The voice test
    • The foot test
    • The song test

  15. Detectives, doctors and scientists all look for tests like this. A test that everybody passes, right or wrong, can't tell you much.

  16. Being right isn't the same as knowing

    Grandma Rose's kitchen clock stopped at exactly three o'clock, but nobody noticed.

  17. One afternoon, Sam glances at it. It happens to be exactly three o'clock. He believes it's three, and he's right.

  18. Does Sam know the time?

    • Yes, because he's right
    • Not really: he's right only by luck
    • Yes, because clocks never lie

  19. His belief is true, but only by luck. The clock would have said "three" whatever the time was.

    So a lucky guess is not knowledge, even when it turns out to be right.

  20. Knowing needs more than being right. It also needs a good reason: one that connects your belief to the truth.

    Little Grape's knowledge was not luck. Her test was built so that a wolf would fail it.

  21. Four ways of holding an idea

    Beliefs can be strong or weak, depending on the reasons behind them.

    • Know: right, for a good reason
    • Believe: think it's true, for some reason
    • Guess: an answer with little or no reason
    • Hope: want it to be true

  23. At the door, the kids asked question 2: how do we know this is Mother?

    Little Grape also asked question 3: what else could explain a soft voice and a white foot? A wolf with honey and flour!

  24. Think about it

    The kids' first two tests could rule the wolf out, but they couldn't rule Mother in. Why not?

    Think of a test in real life that is easy to pass even when the answer is wrong.

    Sam was right about the time. Why do most people say he didn't know it?

    What if the wolf had overheard the song? What new check could the kids make?

  25. How sure are you?

    Clues can make us more sure or less sure. They almost never make us completely sure.

    Say: “better supported” or “still uncertain”. Then ask: What would change my mind?

  26. The big idea

    Knowing needs a true belief and a good reason: evidence that a wrong answer would fail.

  27. New words

    • know: To know something is to be right about it for a good reason, not just by luck.
    • believe: To believe something is to think it is true. A belief can be right or wrong.
    • evidence: Evidence is a clue that helps show whether something is true.
    • reason: A reason is what makes you think something: the "because" behind it.
    • fake: To fake a clue is to make it look true when it isn't, like the wolf's white feet.
    • lucky guess: A lucky guess is right, but only by chance. Being right by luck isn't knowing.
