An old Iranian folk tale, also told by the Brothers Grimm as "The Wolf and the Seven Young Kids". We retold it with a gentler ending.

Knowing or guessing?

Ava’s cookie is gone!

“Sam ate it,” says Max.

Ava asks the most important question on this whole website: “How do you know?”

Max thinks. “Well… Sam loves cookies.”

That is a reason. But is it enough to know? Maybe Sam ate it. Maybe the cat did. Max is guessing.

Then Ava finds crumbs on Sam’s shirt. Now there is a clue. A clue that helps show what is true is called evidence.

Good clues and tricky clues

In the story, the kids used three clues: the voice, the foot and the song.

The voice and the foot were tricky clues. The wolf could fake them with honey and flour.

The song was a good clue. Only Mother knew it. A good clue is one that a faker can’t copy.

Think about it

  • How did Jingle know the first visitor was the wolf?
  • What almost tricked the kids the third time?
  • Why was the secret song a better check than the white foot?

The big idea

To know something, you need a good reason: a clue that is hard to fake.

New words

know

To know something is to be right about it for a good reason, not just by luck.

guess

A guess is an answer you give when you don't have a good reason yet.

hope

To hope is to want something to be true. Wanting it doesn't make it true.

evidence

Evidence is a clue that helps show whether something is true.
