  2. The Fox and the Sweet Grapes

    Grandma Rose Here is a very old story. A storyteller called Aesop told it more than two thousand years ago.

    Once upon a time, a hungry fox trotted past a garden wall.

  3. High above him, on a vine, hung big, round, purple grapes.

    Fox Yum! Those grapes look so sweet!

  4. The fox jumped. He jumped again. He jumped and jumped. But the grapes were too high.

  5. At last he sat down, puffing and panting. Then he lifted his nose and said:

    Fox Hmph! Those grapes are sour anyway. I don't want them!

  6. A little goat was eating grass nearby. Her name was Little Grape.

    Little Grape What makes you say that?

  7. Fox Well… they are very high up. And I am very tired from all that jumping.

    Little Grape But those are reasons about you, not about the grapes. Did you taste one?

    Fox No. I never did.

  8. Little Grape found a ladder by the wall. She held it steady while the fox climbed up and picked one grape. He popped it into his mouth.

    Fox Oh! It's sweet!

  9. The fox said sorry to the grapes, and the two friends ate them in the sun.

    Grandma Rose The fox did not know the grapes were sour. He said "sour" because he could not reach them. So when someone says something, ask: what makes you say that?

  10. A claim and a reason

    Ava's friend says, “That soup is yucky.”

  11. That is a claim.

    A claim is something a person says is true.

  12. “It has carrots. I never tried it.”

    Did he taste the soup? No. His reason is not a good one yet.

  13. About the thing, or about me?

    The fox said, “The grapes are sour.”

    That was his claim.

  14. The fox said the grapes were high. He said he was tired.

    Those reasons are about the fox. They do not tell us how the grapes taste.

  15. Good reasons

    “I tasted one and it was sour.”

    “A bird spat one out.”

    “They are small and green, and green grapes are often sour.”

  16. Excuses

    Sometimes we want something and can't have it.

    Then we say, “I didn't want it anyway!”

  17. That is an excuse. It sounds like a reason, but it does not show the claim is true. A feeling can explain why we say it. It cannot show that it is true.

    Everybody does it. The clever part is to notice.

  18. Ask yourself: Is this a reason about the thing, or about me?

    “I don't like that game. I lost.” Is that about the game, or about me?

    • About me: I feel bad about losing.
    • About the game: it is a bad game.

  19. Think about it

    What was the fox's claim? What were his reasons?

    How could the fox have found out if the grapes were sour?

    Can you think of a time you said “I didn't want it anyway”?

  20. The big idea

    A good reason is about the thing.

    Ask: “What makes you say that?”

  21. New words

    • claim: A claim is something a person says is true.
    • reason: A reason is what makes you think something: the "because" behind it.
    • excuse: An excuse sounds like a reason, but it does not really show that the claim is true.
    • evidence: Evidence is a clue that helps show whether something is true.

  22. Try it

    The fox says, “The grapes are sour.” Is each reason about the grapes, or about the fox? Sort the cards.
