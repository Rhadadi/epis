  2. The Fox and the Sweet Grapes

    Grandma Rose Here is a very old story. A storyteller called Aesop told it more than two thousand years ago.

    Once upon a time, a hungry fox trotted past a garden wall.

  3. High above him, on a vine, hung big, round, purple grapes.

    Fox Yum! Those grapes look so sweet!

  4. The fox jumped. He jumped again. He jumped and jumped. But the grapes were too high.

  5. At last he sat down, puffing and panting. Then he lifted his nose and said:

    Fox Hmph! Those grapes are sour anyway. I don't want them!

  6. A little goat was eating grass nearby. Her name was Little Grape.

    Little Grape What makes you say that?

  7. Fox Well… they are very high up. And I am very tired from all that jumping.

    Little Grape But those are reasons about you, not about the grapes. Did you taste one?

    Fox No. I never did.

  8. Little Grape found a ladder by the wall. She held it steady while the fox climbed up and picked one grape. He popped it into his mouth.

    Fox Oh! It's sweet!

  9. The fox said sorry to the grapes, and the two friends ate them in the sun.

    Grandma Rose The fox did not know the grapes were sour. He said "sour" because he could not reach them. So when someone says something, ask: what makes you say that?

  10. Claims need reasons

    A claim says that something is true. “The grapes are sour.” “This game is boring.” “The bridge is closed.”

  11. A claim stands or falls on its reasons.

    So the best short question is: “What makes you say that?”

  12. It is not rude. It does not say “you're wrong”. It just asks to see the reason, so both of you can look at it together.

  13. The fox's reasons

    The fox's claim: the grapes are sour.

    Little Grape asked what made him say it. He had two reasons: the grapes were high, and he was tired.

  14. Look at what those reasons are about: the fox's position and the fox's energy. They say nothing about grapes.

  15. If the grapes had been sweet, they would be just as high and he would be just as tired.

    A reason that stays the same whether the claim is true or false does not help the claim.

    Which reason would be different if the grapes were sweet?

    • “I tasted one and it was sour.”
    • “They are very high up.”

  16. Sour grapes

    The fox did not lie on purpose. He wanted the grapes and could not get them, and “they are sour” made that feel better.

  17. We build reasons for what we already want to believe. This is called motivated reasoning. The reasons look real, but they were picked after the answer.

  18. It works the other way too. Someone who fails a test may decide the test was “unfair” before looking at it.

  19. A second question joins the first. After “What makes you say that?” ask:

    “Would I say this if I wanted the opposite?”

    If yes, the reason is not carrying any weight.

  20. A fair test

    Little Grape did something even better than asking. She helped the fox check.

    A ladder let him taste one grape, and the claim could finally be tested.

  21. Notice the order: ask for the reason, see that it is weak, then find a test that the claim could fail.

  22. Think about it

    • List the fox's two reasons. Which sentence would still be true if the grapes were sweet?
    • When have you decided something was “boring” or “unfair” before you really tried it?
    • Is it always wrong to say “I don't want it anyway”? When might it be true?

  23. The big idea

    A reason helps a claim only if it would be different when the claim is false.

    Ask: “What makes you say that?” and “Would I say it if I wanted the opposite?”

  24. New words

    • claim: A claim is something a person says is true.
    • reason: A reason is what makes you think something: the "because" behind it.
    • evidence: Evidence is a clue that helps show whether something is true.
    • excuse: An excuse sounds like a reason, but it is really about how we feel.
    • sour grapes: Saying you didn't want something because you couldn't get it.
    • motivated reasoning: Finding reasons for what we already want to be true.

  25. Try it

    “The grapes are sour.” Sort each reason: is it about the grapes, about the fox, or just someone's say-so? Read the explanations.
